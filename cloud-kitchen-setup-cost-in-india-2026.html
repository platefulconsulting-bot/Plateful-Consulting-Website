A small single-brand cloud kitchen setup cost in India in 2026 is roughly ₹4.5 lakh to ₹12 lakh in a tier-2 city and ₹6.3 lakh to ₹18 lakh in a metro, including a refundable rent deposit. Add ₹60,000 to ₹2.1 lakh a month in fixed running costs, and keep cash aside until orders cover them.

These are indicative ranges, not quotes. Rent, deposits and salaries swing widely between cities and even between neighbourhoods. Which licences add to the cloud kitchen cost?

Licences are a small share of the budget, but they can delay your launch. FSSAI fees are low. As per the FoSCoS kind-of-business eligibility list (updated 1 April 2026), a restaurant with annual turnover up to ₹1.5 crore needs basic registration at ₹100 a year. A state licence at ₹5,000 a year applies above ₹1.5 crore up to ₹50 crore. The registration limit was raised from ₹12 lakh, effective 1 April 2026, as explained in this Legal 500 note.

The bigger spends are usually the local ones: trade licence, fire safety clearance where required, shop and establishment registration, and any agent or consultant fees. These differ by state and municipality. Our cloud kitchen licence and registration checklist lists each document and when you need it.

How to budget your cloud kitchen investment: a step-by-step checklist

  1. Fix your cuisine and a short menu first. Equipment, space and staff all follow from it.
  2. Collect three quotes each for rent, civil work, exhaust and equipment in your chosen area.
  3. Confirm the deposit terms and the lock-in period before you pay token money.
  4. List every licence for your state and municipality, with fees and expected timelines.
  5. Cost every dish: ingredients, wastage and packaging, against the price you plan to list.
  6. Get your commission and fee terms in writing, then run them through a per-order sheet.
  7. Work out break-even orders per day from your fixed costs and contribution per order.
  8. Keep a working capital reserve to cover several months of fixed costs and launch ads.
  9. Plan your launch: photos, menu descriptions, opening offers and a small ad budget.
  10. Review actual numbers weekly for the first three months and fix leaks early.

How can you cut cloud kitchen setup cost?

  • Start inside an existing restaurant. If you already run a kitchen with spare capacity, adding a delivery brand avoids most of the deposit, civil work and equipment cost. Check that your licences and platform listings cover the new brand.
  • Use a shared or managed kitchen. Some operators rent a ready kitchen in a shared facility. You pay less upfront, though the rent or revenue share can be higher. Compare total cost over a year, not just day one.
  • Buy used equipment. Closed restaurants often sell ranges, chillers and steel tables. Check burners, compressors and gas fittings before paying.
  • Keep the menu tight. Fewer items mean less equipment, less dead stock and simpler training. Redseer also lists focused menus as a trait of successful brands.
  • Phase your spending. Buy only what the launch menu needs. Add equipment when orders justify it.
  • Test from home first. For very small menus, a home kitchen on Swiggy and Zomato can prove demand before you lease a space.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a small cloud kitchen in India?

For a single-brand, delivery-only kitchen of about 250 to 400 sq ft, our indicative range is ₹4.5 lakh to ₹12 lakh in a tier-2 city and ₹6.3 lakh to ₹18 lakh in a metro. This includes a refundable deposit, equipment, exhaust, licences, stock, packaging and branding. It excludes working capital, which you should keep separately to cover the first few months of fixed costs.

What is a typical cloud kitchen profit margin?

A Redseer analysis reported by Business Today in July 2026 put cloud kitchen EBITDA margins at about 12%, compared with about 8% for dine-in restaurants. Your own margin depends on commission terms, food cost, discounts, ad spend and order volume. Many small kitchens lose money in early months because fixed costs are spread over too few orders.

How many orders a day does a cloud kitchen need to break even?

Divide your monthly fixed costs by your contribution per order, then by 30. In our illustration, a metro kitchen with ₹1.5 lakh of fixed costs and ₹94 contribution per order needs about 53 orders a day. A tier-2 kitchen with ₹90,000 of fixed costs needs about 32. Your numbers will differ, so run them with your actual contract and recipe costs.

How much is the FSSAI fee for a cloud kitchen?

According to the FoSCoS eligibility list updated on 1 April 2026, a restaurant with turnover up to ₹1.5 crore needs basic FSSAI registration at ₹100 per year. Above ₹1.5 crore and up to ₹50 crore, a state licence applies at ₹5,000 per year. Other local permits such as trade licence and fire clearance vary by state and city.

Is it cheaper to start a cloud kitchen inside an existing restaurant?

Usually, yes. An existing kitchen already has equipment, exhaust, staff and often licences, so a new delivery brand mainly needs menu development, packaging, photography and listing work. The risk is overloading the kitchen at peak hours, which can hurt prep times and ratings for both brands. Check your licence and platform listing rules before launching the second brand.
