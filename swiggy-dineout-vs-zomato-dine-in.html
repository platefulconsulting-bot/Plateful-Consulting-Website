There is no single winner. Swiggy usually brings more delivery frequency and is stronger in Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Chennai. Zomato brings more discovery, higher order values and dine-in intent, and is stronger in Delhi NCR and tier-2 cities. Base commission is similar on both, so most restaurants should list on both and pick one primary platform for ad spend.

If you run a restaurant in India, you have probably had this conversation: focus on Swiggy, go all-in on Zomato, or manage both? It is a fair question. Ad budget spent on one platform does not carry over to the other, and with commission and fees taking a large share of every order, a wrong bet can drain margin for months before anyone notices.

This guide does not take sides. It puts the numbers that matter next to each other (commission, ranking signals, city strength, ads and restaurant fit) so you can decide from your own data rather than from platform sales pitches. How Swiggy and Zomato Differ for Restaurant Partners

Swiggy and Zomato are India's two dominant food delivery aggregators. Both work on the same basic model: you list your menu, they bring the demand and handle delivery, and you pay commission and fees on each completed order. The difference is who that demand is, how customers find you, and what it costs to serve them.

Swiggy launched in 2014 as a delivery-first platform and built its own last-mile fleet early, which gave it a strong record on delivery time. Instamart (quick commerce) and Dineout (dining out) are its main expansion verticals.

Zomato launched in 2008 as a restaurant discovery platform before moving into delivery. That history gives it stronger recall for dining out, especially in North India and tier-2 cities. Blinkit and Hyperpure are its parallel businesses.

Commission: What Each Platform Actually Takes

Both platforms charge commission on order value (GMV), not on profit. Take a ₹400 order at 25% commission: ₹100 goes to the platform before you count food cost, packaging, rent or staff. Then 18% GST is added on the commission itself, and a payment or collection fee of about 2% plus GST comes on top.

Neither platform publishes a rate card. Swiggy describes commission as set on terms "pre-agreed" with each restaurant (Swiggy Annual Report 2025-26), and Zomato sets it per restaurant in each partner's form (Zomato partner terms). Press reports put the market range at about 16–30%. The slab rows below are the ranges we see across PFC client contracts.

Where restaurants lose most is the ads layer: new and mid-tier restaurants rarely hold visibility on organic ranking alone, and once paid placements are added, the effective take rate can approach 40% of GMV. How Swiggy and Zomato Rank Restaurants

Visibility drives order volume, and neither platform publishes its ranking algorithm. The NRAI has publicly criticised these "algorithmic blind spots", and says 30% of restaurants get 75–80% of orders. The patterns below come from the restaurant listings we audit.

Swiggy ranking signals (operations-driven)

  • Delivery time accuracy: restaurants that meet their quoted time rank higher.
  • Acceptance rate: cancellations and rejections pull ranking down.
  • Recent order volume: the algorithm weights recent performance, not your all-time total.
  • Rating: in our audits, around 4.0 is the practical minimum to hold a strong position in your category.
  • Menu completeness: items with photos, descriptions and prices perform better.
  • Ad spend: paid placements support organic signals but do not replace them.

Zomato ranking signals (perception-driven)

  • Ratings and reviews: Zomato looks at review quality, not just the star average.
  • Pricing against local competitors: value perception matters.
  • Photo quality: the discovery-led app rewards good food photography.
  • Cuisine tag accuracy: wrong tags keep you out of relevant searches.
  • Engagement: clicks and saves on your listing.
  • Operational consistency: unplanned closures and unavailable items count against you.

The practical difference: on Swiggy, fix delivery time and acceptance rate and ranking usually improves within 2–3 weeks in our experience. On Zomato, better photos, reviews and price positioning compound over months. Our deep dives on the Swiggy ranking algorithm and Zomato ranking cover each signal.

Should You Be on Both Swiggy and Zomato?

For most restaurants, yes, but with deliberate allocation rather than equal effort. There is no upfront listing cost in the accounts we see (you pay on completed orders), and the extra work is mainly menu management, order handling and customer support, since both platforms run delivery. In the restaurants we have worked with, those active on both have typically shown 35–55% higher total aggregator GMV than single-platform peers in the same category and city (illustrative; results vary).

The risk is spreading promotion too thin and ending up mid-table on both. The approach that works:

  • Pick a primary platform based on your city and restaurant type.
  • Put 60–70% of your marketing budget and menu work there.
  • Keep the second platform active, accurate and well-rated on a smaller budget.
  • Review the split every quarter as your numbers change.

Levers That Work on Both Platforms

Platform choice is one variable. Restaurants that grow aggregator revenue apply these levers whichever platform they prioritise.

1. Build a delivery menu for order value

Your delivery menu should not be a copy of your dine-in menu. Strong delivery menus are shorter (20–30 items), built around combos that lift cart value, and photographed for a phone screen. In our client work, restaurants that rebuild the delivery menu around what sells at a margin often see AOV improve by 25–40% within 60 days (illustrative; results vary). More in menu optimisation for delivery.

2. Get the photos right

In our audits, listings with professional food photos get noticeably more clicks than text-only listings in the same category. Both platforms offer photography programmes. If you use them, brief the photographer for top-down dish shots that work on delivery apps, not ambience shots.

3. Recover ratings at the source

A rating below 4.0 hurts visibility on both apps. Recovery is not mainly about replying to reviews. It is about finding the operational failures behind them (late orders, poor packaging, wrong items) and fixing them in the kitchen.

4. Review commission through official channels

Both platforms set commission per contract, and higher-volume partners often move to lower rates. In our experience, restaurants doing ₹3–5 lakh monthly GMV on a platform are often in a position to ask for a lower rate, sometimes 2–5 percentage points below where they started. This is a conversation with your official partner or account manager, backed by your volume, ratings and outlet count, not a support ticket. No outcome is guaranteed, and it is worth revisiting every 6–12 months.

5. Know who funds each discount

Some offers are partly platform-funded and some come almost entirely out of your margin. In our experience, Buy 1 Get 1 offers are mostly restaurant-funded, while platform campaigns are shared. Zomato's terms say restaurant-funded promotions apply only where you have agreed to them (Zomato partner terms), so check the funding split in writing before you switch any offer on. Frequently Asked Questions

Which is more profitable for restaurants, Swiggy or Zomato?

It depends on your commission rate, fees, ad spend and order volume, not the platform name. Base commission is similar on both, and across PFC client accounts the effective take rate with GST, payment fees and ads usually lands around 25–40% of GMV. Track cost per order, AOV and net margin per platform for 60 days, then put more budget where the margin is better.

Is Swiggy or Zomato commission lower?

Base commission bands are almost the same: typically 18–28% in the contracts we audit, plus 18% GST on the commission. The fees on top differ. Swiggy charges a 2% collection fee and, for some partners, a Swiggy One order fee; Zomato charges a payment mechanism fee and a long-distance fee on longer deliveries. The bigger difference usually comes from how efficiently you spend on ads.

Should my restaurant be on both Swiggy and Zomato?

For most restaurants, yes. There is no upfront listing cost in the accounts we see and both platforms handle delivery, so the extra work is menu and order management. Choose one primary platform for most of your ad budget and menu work, and keep the second active and well-rated on a smaller budget.

Which has more orders and users, Swiggy or Zomato?

Both are national-scale platforms. In our client accounts, Swiggy leads in South India, especially Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Chennai, while Zomato is stronger in Delhi NCR and many tier-2 cities. For planning, your city, cuisine and delivery radius matter more than national totals.

How can I improve my ranking on both Swiggy and Zomato?

On Swiggy, improve delivery time accuracy, cut cancellations and keep your rating above 4.0. On Zomato, add professional dish photos, fix cuisine tags, respond to reviews and keep pricing competitive. Both reward consistency, and in our experience ranking usually recovers within 3–6 weeks of fixing the underlying problem.

Is Swiggy Dineout or Zomato better for dine-in restaurants?

For dine-in discovery, Zomato generally has the edge because of its roots in restaurant discovery: customers use it to decide where to eat, compare menus and check reviews. Swiggy's Dineout is its dining-out vertical and is worth keeping accurate too. Our Zomato dine-in growth guide covers table bookings and walk-ins in detail.
