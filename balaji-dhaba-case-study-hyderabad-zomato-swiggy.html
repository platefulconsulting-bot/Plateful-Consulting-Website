Balaji Dhaba, a dhaba-style restaurant in Marredpally, Secunderabad, grew its average weekly Zomato delivered orders from about 236 to about 796 (3.4x) over about a year while working with Plateful Consulting. In five months, monthly Zomato sales rose 60% to ₹12.17 lakh, and Swiggy ads returned 16.6x ROAS over about ten weeks.

This is our first published Hyderabad case study, and the owner has approved being named. Every figure below comes from Balaji Dhaba's own Zomato and Swiggy partner dashboards. Results vary; this is one client's outcome, not a guarantee.

Where Balaji Dhaba started

Balaji Dhaba serves classic North Indian dhaba food to Secunderabad and nearby Hyderabad neighbourhoods. When the tracked period began, it was already a working delivery kitchen with steady demand: roughly 220 to 260 delivered orders a week on Zomato, about ₹7.60 lakh in monthly Zomato sales and an average order value of ₹370.

The base was healthy but flat. Impressions were reaching people, yet only 6.3% of those impressions turned into menu visits, and 7.6% of menu visitors placed an order. For a dhaba competing with hundreds of North Indian listings in Hyderabad, those two numbers decide how much of the platform's traffic actually becomes revenue.

What changed on Zomato

Over about a year, weekly orders did not rise in a straight line. They stepped up in stages, with each level holding before the next climb:

  • Stage one: about 220 to 260 orders a week
  • Stage two: about 300 to 360 orders a week
  • Stage three: about 450 to 550 orders a week
  • Stage four: about 650 to 830 orders a week

The average across the latest stretch came to about 796 orders a week, 3.4x the starting average of about 236. The best single week reached 985 delivered orders.

What changed on Swiggy

On Swiggy, we have about ten weeks of dashboard data. In that window, Balaji Dhaba did ₹37.5 lakh in revenue from 7,363 orders, and only 8 orders were rejected (0.1%).

Swiggy ads and efficiency

  • Ads spend of ₹1.04 lakh generated ₹17.2 lakh in ads GMV, a 16.6x ROAS over the period.
  • Monthly ROAS moved from 15.8x to 16.6x to 18.7x.
  • Ad-driven orders grew from 112 to 464 a week.
  • Discount spend ran at about 24% of revenue and came down to 22.1%.

Menu and operations on Swiggy

Every menu item had a description (0% missing), only 4.7% of items lacked an image, and 15% of menu visitors ordered. Availability stayed at 99% and average prep time at 10 minutes.

Conversion before ads: why menu-to-order matters

The single most useful number in this case is menu-to-order conversion on Zomato, which moved from 7.6% to 8.8%, and to 10.7% in the latest partial month. Think about what that means in practice. If 40,000 people open your menu in a month, the difference between 7.6% and 8.8% is about 480 extra orders, without one extra rupee of ad spend.

Ads buy attention. The menu converts it. When the menu score climbed from 86.5% to nearly 90%, and Swiggy items had full descriptions and almost complete images, each visitor brought in by ads or by search had a better chance of ordering. That is also why rising ad spend did not destroy efficiency here: Swiggy ROAS improved month by month instead of falling as spend scaled.

Average order value moving from ₹370 to ₹401 adds to the same effect. More orders at a higher basket size compound into the 60% sales lift.

What other Hyderabad restaurants can take from this

We are not going to pretend there is one trick here. What the numbers show is a pattern that any dhaba or North Indian kitchen in Hyderabad can check against its own dashboard:

  1. Look at your funnel, not just your orders. Impressions to menu and menu to order tell you whether the problem is reach or conversion. Balaji Dhaba improved both.
  2. Fix menu completeness first. A menu score near 90%, every item described and nearly every item photographed gives ads something worth sending traffic to.
  3. Judge ads on ROAS, not on spend. Swiggy ROAS rose from 15.8x to 18.7x. Rising efficiency is a sign the targeting and menu are working together.
  4. Watch discount share. Discount spend fell from about 24% to 22.1% of revenue while orders grew. Growth does not have to mean deeper discounts.
  5. Protect operations as volume grows. 98 to 100% availability, 8 to 10 minute prep and a 0.1% rejection rate on Swiggy meant the extra demand was actually fulfilled.
  6. Expect steps, not a straight line. Weekly Zomato orders climbed in four distinct levels. Plateaus are normal; the question is what moves you to the next one.
