Swiggy does not publish its ranking formula, but in our audits it consistently rewards reliability: order acceptance, prep-time accuracy, ratings and recent order volume, menu completeness, availability at peak hours and low complaint rates. Restaurants that fix these operational signals usually see their ranking move within 2–4 weeks, especially when acceptance and delivery performance improve.

Restaurants that consistently meet delivery promises, maintain high ratings and accept orders reliably appear higher in search results and category listings.

If your restaurant is listed on Swiggy but receives inconsistent order volume, the problem is rarely the food quality alone. In most cases, the underlying issue is visibility inside Swiggy's discovery system.

Customers rarely scroll beyond the first few listings in any category. Orders are heavily concentrated: the NRAI says 30% of restaurants get 75–80% of orders, and in our audits the top 10–15 results in a category take most of the volume.

Understanding how Swiggy's ranking works is therefore not just a marketing exercise. It is directly tied to revenue on the platform.

The signals behind this visibility are surprisingly consistent.

What the Swiggy Algorithm Actually Measures

Swiggy's ranking system appears designed to favour restaurants that create predictable delivery experiences for customers.

Swiggy does not publish its algorithm, and the NRAI has publicly criticised how opaque platform visibility is; patterns across the hundreds of listings we have audited show that Swiggy evaluates performance across three broad categories:

  1. Operational reliability
  2. Customer satisfaction
  3. Delivery speed

Within those categories, several specific signals influence ranking.

Each of these signals influences how often your restaurant appears in search results, cuisine listings and recommendation feeds.

Factor 1: Order Acceptance Rate

One of the strongest signals we see is order acceptance rate (OAR). It measures how reliably a restaurant accepts incoming orders.

Example:

Orders receivedOrders acceptedAcceptance rate
1009595%

Restaurants that frequently reject or cancel orders signal operational instability.

In our audits, listings with acceptance rates below 90% often drop significantly in ranking. Treat that as an observed threshold, not a published rule.

Operational fix

Stay online only when the kitchen can reliably fulfil orders. Temporarily pausing the listing during overload is better than rejecting orders repeatedly.

Factor 2: Ratings and Review Velocity

Customer ratings influence Swiggy visibility, but not just through the average rating number. In our experience, review velocity, the rate at which new reviews come in, also matters.

As an illustration, a restaurant with:

  • Rating: 4.4
  • Reviews: 200

can rank lower than a restaurant with:

  • Rating: 4.3
  • Reviews: 1,200 recent reviews

because frequent customer engagement signals active demand and satisfaction.

In our audits, a rating above 4.2 is generally needed to stay competitive in most food categories. For a step-by-step recovery plan, see how to lift your Swiggy rating.

Factor 3: Preparation Time Accuracy

Swiggy tracks whether restaurants prepare orders within the time they promise.

If the app shows a 15-minute preparation estimate but the restaurant consistently needs 25 minutes, delivery reliability drops. This creates:

  • Rider waiting time
  • Delivery delays
  • Customer dissatisfaction

In our experience, restaurants that consistently match their preparation estimates perform noticeably better in ranking.

Practical optimisation

  • Set prep time estimates realistically
  • Pre-prepare high-volume items
  • Improve the kitchen dispatch workflow

Factor 4: Delivery Time Performance

Delivery time is one of the biggest drivers of customer choice on delivery platforms. In our audits, Swiggy tends to favour restaurants that can deliver faster within the same cuisine category.

Delivery speed depends on several operational variables:

Operational factorImpact
Delivery radiusA larger radius increases delays
Prep time alignmentRider waiting time increases delivery time
Order dispatch timingDelays compound during peak hours

Restaurants that optimise these variables typically see ranking improvements within weeks.

Factor 5: Menu Conversion Rate

Swiggy can see how customers behave after viewing your listing: how many people view the menu and then place an order. This is your menu conversion rate.

A low conversion rate suggests customers do not find the listing attractive or competitive.

Conversion improvements

Restaurants can improve conversion by:

  • Adding high-quality food photography
  • Highlighting bestselling items
  • Structuring combo offers
  • Writing clear menu descriptions

In the listings we work on, better conversion tends to bring more exposure in recommendations. Our Swiggy listing optimisation guide covers this in detail.

Factor 6: Restaurant Availability

Restaurants that frequently go offline or pause orders during peak hours see their ranking decline. Swiggy appears to prioritise restaurants that stay reliably available during high-demand periods.

Typical peak windows include:

Time slotDemand pattern
Lunch (12–3 PM)Office and weekday orders
Dinner (7–11 PM)Highest daily demand
WeekendsHigh frequency

Restaurants missing these windows lose significant visibility.

Factor 7: Customer Complaint Rate

Swiggy tracks operational complaints including:

  • Missing items
  • Incorrect orders
  • Packaging failures

Listings with frequent complaints see reduced visibility. Complaints and cancellations can also cost money directly: in 2026, Bengaluru restaurant associations objected to deductions that platforms make on such orders.

Reducing complaints usually needs process improvements rather than customer communication. Common fixes include:

  • Order verification checklists
  • Packaging improvements
  • Menu accuracy updates

The Swiggy restaurant ranking algorithm rewards reliability above everything else.

Frequently asked questions

Does Swiggy publish its ranking algorithm?

No. Swiggy does not publish how it ranks restaurants, and industry body NRAI has criticised how little visibility restaurants get into it. The signals in this guide come from patterns across the listings we audit.

What acceptance rate do I need on Swiggy?

There is no published minimum. In our audits, listings that fall below about 90% acceptance often lose ranking noticeably, so aim to stay well above that and pause the listing rather than reject orders when the kitchen is overloaded.

What rating do I need to rank well on Swiggy?

In our audits, a rating above 4.2 is generally needed to stay competitive in most categories. Recent review volume matters too, so a steady flow of good reviews helps more than a high but stale average.

How long does it take for Swiggy ranking to improve?

In our client work, ranking usually starts moving within 2–4 weeks of fixing operations: about 1–2 weeks for acceptance fixes, 2–3 weeks for delivery time and 3–4 weeks for rating recovery. Results vary.

Can Swiggy ads fix a poor ranking?

Not on their own. Ads buy visibility while they run, but if acceptance, prep time or ratings are weak, the extra traffic converts poorly and organic ranking does not hold once spend stops. Fix the fundamentals first, then add ads.
