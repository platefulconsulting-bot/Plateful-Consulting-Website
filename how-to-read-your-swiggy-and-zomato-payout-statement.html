Read a Swiggy or Zomato payout statement from menu value down to bank payout, one line at a time: restaurant-funded discounts, commission plus 18% GST, payment or collection fees plus GST, ads, refunds and TDS. Check each line against your agreement, POS and ads dashboard. In our worked example, a restaurant on 24% commission keeps about 56% of menu value.

Commission is only the first deduction. Between the menu value of your orders and the money that reaches your bank, a payout statement typically subtracts:

  • restaurant-funded discounts
  • commission, plus 18% GST on it
  • payment or collection charges, plus GST
  • ad spend billed against payouts
  • refunds and deductions for customer complaints
  • TDS under the e-commerce rules

Reading the statement line by line is the only way to know your real take rate. Most owners never do it.

Swiggy and Zomato label things differently and update formats from time to time, but every weekly statement is built from the same components:

Zomato's partner terms confirm several of these lines: its fees are exclusive of taxes, it charges a separate payment mechanism fee, and refunds are adjusted before the weekly settlement. Its Growth Pack ad terms provide for ad invoices to be settled by deduction from weekly payouts. Swiggy's 2% collection fee is reported by Free Press Journal.

One item that is not your cost: the 5% GST on food. Since 1 January 2022 the platform collects it from the customer and pays it to the government on your behalf, as Zomato's partner terms confirm, so it should pass through the statement without reducing your earnings.

The headline commission is 24%, but ₹87,244 of the ₹2,00,000 menu value, or 43.6%, never reaches the bank, in line with the NRAI's estimate that restaurants give away 40–45% of order value once all costs are counted. Some of that may come back: TDS is credited against your income tax, and GST on fees may be claimable as input tax credit depending on your GST position (check with your CA). The rest is the real cost of the channel, and it is the number your menu prices have to be built on.

Commission base varies by contract. Zomato's terms, for example, define gross sales as net of restaurant-funded discounts, which is why the example applies 24% to ₹1,80,000 rather than ₹2,00,000. Check which base your own agreement uses.

Calculate Your Real Take Rate

Use two numbers from every statement:

Effective take rate = (menu value − bank payout) ÷ menu value

In the example: (₹2,00,000 − ₹1,12,756) ÷ ₹2,00,000 = 43.6%. Then separate it into the parts you control:

  • Commission and its GST: agreed at contract time and mostly fixed between reviews.
  • Payment or collection fee: a fixed percentage on every order; check the rate is the one you agreed.
  • Discounts: fully in your control. This is usually the largest lever. See which Swiggy and Zomato offers make money.
  • Ads: in your control, and only worth it at a positive return.
  • Refunds and penalties: an operations problem: packaging, missing items, late dispatch.

If your effective take rate is above 40%, the fix is almost never "negotiate commission". It is discounts, ads and refunds. For the commission side specifically, see how to reduce Swiggy commission impact on margins and what restaurants actually pay Zomato.

Understanding TDS on Your Statement

Swiggy and Zomato deduct TDS as e-commerce operators. The rate is 0.1% since 1 October 2024 under the e-commerce TDS provisions (Section 194-O of the Income-tax Act, 1961 until March 2026; the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced it from 1 April 2026 and carries the e-commerce TDS forward). Section numbers and form names may have changed under the new Act, so have your CA confirm the current references.

There is also a second TDS flow that many owners miss. Zomato's partner terms provide for the restaurant to deduct TDS on the service fee it pays Zomato, with that amount refunded to the restaurant against the quarterly TDS certificate furnished within 60 days. If this applies to you, track those certificates as part of your reconciliation.

A Weekly Reconciliation Routine

  1. Match order counts. Delivered orders on the statement should match delivered orders in your POS or kitchen log for the same days.
  2. Match the menu value. A gap usually means price mismatches between the app menu and your POS, or orders marked delivered that you never prepared.
  3. Check every discount line. Confirm each offer was one you opted into and that the funding share is what you agreed.
  4. Check the fee lines. Commission and payment or collection fees should match the rates in your agreement.
  5. Review refunds one by one. Pull the order, check the complaint, and raise a dispute for any you can disprove, such as with a dispatch photo or packing checklist.
  6. Tie ads to campaigns. The ads deduction should equal what the ads dashboard shows for the week.
  7. File the TDS. Check quarterly that the platform's TDS shows up against your PAN in your annual tax statement, and that any TDS certificates you owe or are owed are in place.

Payouts are weekly; Zomato's terms provide for weekly remittance, or within 3 bank working days if you opt for it (Zomato partner terms). In our experience the routine takes about 30 minutes a week with a spreadsheet and both dashboards open. Disputes are much harder to win once time has passed, so the routine only works if you run it every week.

Red Flags to Escalate

  • Commission or fees charged at a rate or on a base that differs from your agreement.
  • Discounts deducted for offers you did not opt into, or at a higher funding share, one of the issues Bengaluru restaurant associations raised in 2026.
  • A rising refund line with no matching rise in complaints on your end.
  • Ads deductions that exceed dashboard spend, or ad charges you did not agree to in writing.
  • TDS deducted but missing from your annual tax statement.

Raise these through the partner support channel with order IDs and screenshots. In our audits, recovered amounts from a single persistent error have added up to more than a month of ad budget.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my Swiggy or Zomato payout so much lower than my sales?

Because commission is only one of several deductions. Discounts, GST on commission, payment or collection fees, ads, refunds and TDS all come out before the bank transfer. In our illustrative example, 43.6% of menu value is deducted on a 24% commission.

How much TDS do Swiggy and Zomato deduct from restaurant payouts?

As e-commerce operators they deduct TDS at 0.1% since October 2024. It is not a cost: it is credited against your income-tax liability, provided your PAN is linked and the deduction shows in your annual tax statement. The Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect on 1 April 2026, so confirm section references and form names with your CA.

Do restaurants pay GST on Swiggy and Zomato orders?

Not on the food itself. The platform collects and deposits the 5% GST on restaurant food it delivers. You do pay 18% GST on the platform's commission and fees. Whether you can claim it as input tax credit depends on your GST position, so check with your CA.

Is the payment or collection fee part of commission?

No. Swiggy charges a 2% collection fee and Zomato charges a payment mechanism fee set in your contract, each with 18% GST. They appear as separate lines and add roughly 2.4 points to your take rate at a 2% rate.

How often should I reconcile my aggregator payouts?

Weekly, in line with the payout cycle. Small weekly gaps are much easier to trace and dispute than a quarter's worth at once.
