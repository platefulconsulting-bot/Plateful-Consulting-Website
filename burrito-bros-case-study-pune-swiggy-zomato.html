The starting point: a 1.8 rating blocking everything else

When The Burrito Bros came on board, the Zomato rating stood at 1.8. A rating that low makes every other lever weak. Customers scroll past, conversion from menu visits stays poor, and any money put into ads buys clicks that do not turn into orders.

So the plan was sequenced deliberately: fix the rating first, make the menu and kitchen delivery-ready, and only then push ad spend. Rating repair, then menu, then ads, then a second brand.

One important caveat on the numbers: The Burrito Bros joined partway through the first month, so Month 1 is the onboarding month, not a full "before PFC" month. No pre-PFC data was shared with us. Every multiple in this post (7.6x, 10.7x, 10.5x) compares the sixth month with that onboarding month, and you should read them that way.

What PFC did, step by step

1. Rating repair first

PFC ran rating drives and handled customer complaints on Zomato. The rating moved from 1.8 in the onboarding month to 4.1 by the third month and 4.3 by the sixth month. If your own outlet is stuck below 4, our guide on how to improve Zomato ratings covers the tactics in more detail.

2. A delivery-ready menu with basket-size levers

The menu was cleaned up on both apps. On Zomato, Dish-bytes videos were added to the listing. To lift basket size and give price-sensitive customers a reason to order, the outlet used:

  • Flash sales
  • ₹99 items as low-ticket entry points
  • A freebie on orders above ₹399

Average order value settled between ₹271 and ₹300 from the second month onwards, even as order volume grew several times over. The onboarding month showed ₹425, but on only 132 orders.

3. Ads scaled only on returns

Ad spend went up only as returns held. Monthly Zomato ad spend grew from ₹5.5K to ₹63K, while Zomato ad ROI rose from 2.4x to the 5–6x range, as reported by the platforms' ad dashboards. Growing spend this much without the return collapsing is the clearest sign that the rating and menu work had done its job first.

4. Kitchen speed and zero lost orders

Zomato prep time came down from 14 minutes to 5–8 minutes. From the fourth month onwards, the outlet recorded zero lost sales and zero cancellations on both Swiggy and Zomato. Faster, reliable fulfilment protects the rating that had just been rebuilt.

5. A second brand from the same kitchen

PFC also launched Taco Amigos as a second delivery brand from the same kitchen, live and reporting by the fourth month. Its numbers are excluded from every figure in this case study, so the results above are The Burrito Bros brand alone.

6. Clean handover

At the end of the plan, all logins, IDs and the full discount list were handed back to the owner.

Why Swiggy dipped mid-plan

Zomato grew every single month. Swiggy did not. Swiggy sales fell in the fourth and fifth months, from ₹95,183 to ₹85,089 and then ₹60,102, and Swiggy orders dropped from 351 to 187.

The cause was on Swiggy's side: a duplicate-outlet visibility issue that reduced the outlet's visibility while it was being fixed with Swiggy. The issue was resolved towards the end of the sixth month, and Swiggy sales recovered to ₹93,152 that month. We are calling this out because a dip like this can look like a strategy failure on a chart when it is really a listing problem. If your Swiggy numbers fall while Zomato holds steady, check visibility and listing health before changing anything else.

What did not go perfectly

  • Complaints rose with volume. Zomato complaints went from 2 to 23 a month as monthly orders grew from 132 to 1,417. Complaint handling has to scale with the order count, or the rating work gets undone.
  • Swiggy lost two months to the visibility issue described above.
  • The final month's Swiggy ad ROI was reported as an outlier and is not used anywhere in this case study.

The takeaway for restaurant owners

The Burrito Bros result was not about one clever ad. It came from doing things in the right order. A low rating caps everything, so it was fixed first. Then the menu, basket-size offers and kitchen speed made each order count. Only after that did ad spend go up, and it went up because returns justified it. Once ratings and operations were sorted, the growth compounded.
