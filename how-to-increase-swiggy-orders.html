To increase Swiggy orders in 30 days, work on five levers in order: fix your listing, rebuild your menu for delivery, manage ratings actively, run structured Swiggy ads, and price for commission. Most of the early gain comes from the listing, menu and ratings, which cost little. Ads then speed up what those fixes have started.

Most restaurants on Swiggy are stuck in the same loop: decent food, uneven orders, and no clear view of what is pulling customers in or pushing them away. Swiggy works like a performance marketplace, not a passive order channel. Listings that turn more views into orders get shown more, and listings that slip lose position gradually.

This guide sets out what to fix, in what order, and why, based on what we implement for 650+ restaurants on Swiggy and Zomato across India.

Why Most Restaurants Struggle to Grow on Swiggy

Many owners assume a Swiggy listing brings orders on its own. In practice, every restaurant in your delivery radius is competing on three things:

  • Visibility: how high you appear in search and category lists.
  • Conversion: how many people who open your menu actually order.
  • Customer satisfaction: ratings, complaints and repeat orders.

Visibility matters because orders are heavily concentrated: the NRAI says 30% of restaurants get 75–80% of orders. Swiggy does not publish its ranking algorithm, so the signals below come from the listings we audit. Each of the five steps improves one or more of them.

Step 1: Fix Your Listing Before Anything Else

Your listing is your shop window. Before you spend a rupee on ads or offers, the listing has to work, because a weak listing wastes every paid click you send to it.

Cuisine tags

Match your tags to what customers actually search. If you serve North Indian food but your tag only says Indian, you miss a large search segment. Swiggy allows multiple tags, so use all that genuinely apply.

Hero image and dish photos

In our audits, the hero image is the biggest single driver of click-through on Swiggy. A dark or blurry photo costs orders however good the food is. Get professional shots of your top 10 dishes first. In our experience, listings with strong visuals typically get 2–3x the click-through rate of weak ones.

Operating hours

Make sure your listed hours match when you are really accepting orders. Orders missed during live hours hurt your acceptance rate, which in our audits is one of the clearest ranking signals on Swiggy.

Minimum order value

Set your minimum order value at what a single person realistically orders in your category, not what suits your margin. A minimum that is too high sends customers to competitors. Recover margin through the menu instead.

Menu hygiene

Remove items you cannot serve, and make sure your best sellers are clearly marked. Unavailable items frustrate customers and count against you.

Step 2: Engineer Your Menu for Delivery

A restaurant menu and a delivery menu are different things. Uploading your dine-in menu as it is one of the most common mistakes we see. A delivery menu has three jobs: get the click, get the item into the cart, and lift order value.

Keep it short

Restaurants with 80-item menus on Swiggy consistently underperform those with a focused list of roughly 20–35 items in our experience. A shorter menu reduces decision fatigue and speeds up the kitchen, which helps ratings.

Lead with best sellers that make money

Swiggy shows your top-performing items first, so make sure they are also high-margin items. If they are not, that is a pricing problem, not a product problem.

Write descriptions that sell

Most restaurants leave the description blank or write something generic. A good description names the key ingredient, the flavour and the portion size.

Use combos

A well-built combo (main, side and drink) is the most reliable way to raise order value. In our client work, restaurants that introduce structured combos often lift AOV by 25–40% (illustrative; results vary). Price the combo so the customer feels they save about 15–20% against buying items separately.

Structure the menu

Strong delivery menus usually follow the same order: best sellers, combo meals, core items, add-ons, then beverages or desserts. Choose items that are easy to prepare consistently and travel well in packaging.

Step 3: Manage Your Ratings Actively

Your rating is the most visible trust signal on your listing. Below 4.0, visibility and click-through usually drop noticeably. Ratings move when the experience becomes predictable, so start with the causes.

Wrong or missing itemsRating dropsAdd an order check before handover

  • Reply to reviews, positive and negative, within 24 hours, and keep the tone professional.
  • For negative reviews, resolve the issue privately through Swiggy support where you can.
  • Fix packaging first. Food arriving cold, spilled or crushed is the most common reason for bad reviews.
  • Ask happy customers for a rating with a simple packaging insert, such as "Loved your order? Rate us on Swiggy."

Step 4: Run Swiggy Ads the Right Way

Advertising is now a major revenue line for Swiggy, and its 2025-26 annual report says restaurant partners are increasingly using its self-serve visibility tools. Most listing ads we run for clients are billed per click, but confirm the billing basis in your Partner app before you start. Most restaurants either ignore ads or spend without a plan. The common mistakes are a daily budget that is too high, promoting items that do not sell, and not tracking cost per order.

Weeks 1–2: visibility

Run a low-budget campaign of about ₹100–200 a day on your top three dishes and your restaurant name. The aim here is impressions and early data, not maximum orders.

Weeks 3–4: conversion

Raise the budget to about ₹300–500 a day and move toward high-intent searches for your cuisine. Promote best sellers, combos and high-margin items. Check ROAS and cost per order weekly. In our client campaigns, above 4x ROAS is healthy for most restaurant categories, but results vary and are not guaranteed. ROAS is measured on revenue, so with commission and fees taking a quarter or more of each order, a low ROAS can still lose money.

What not to do

  • Do not run ads while your rating is below 3.8.
  • Do not promote dishes with long prep times during peak hours.
  • Do not set and forget. Check every three days and pause what is not converting.
  • Do not skip the payout check. Match the ads line on your payout statement to your dashboard every week; in 2026, Bengaluru restaurant associations objected to ad charges deducted without written consent.

Step 5: Fix Your Pricing Structure

Delivery pricing is not dine-in pricing. In the contracts we see, Swiggy commission typically sits between 18% and 27% of order value before GST; press reports put it at 17–25% plus a 2% collection fee. Customers can sort by price against dozens of similar restaurants.

Pricing moveBenefit
Price delivery 10–15% above dine-inCovers packaging, commission and fees
Bundle pricing (combos)Lifts AOV
Price anchoring: one premium item at the top of each categoryMakes mid-tier items, where margin sits, look better value
Test ₹199 and ₹299 price points for single-serve itemsStrong conversion bands in most Indian cities

Going more than 20% above dine-in is where price-sensitive customers start to leave. Avoid identical dine-in and delivery pricing, heavy discounting, and inconsistent pricing across similar items. Our guide on reducing the impact of Swiggy commission shows the per-order maths.

Your 30-Day Plan, Week by Week

  1. Week 1: Fix listing photos, cuisine tags and hours. Restructure menu categories and identify your best sellers.
  2. Week 2: Introduce combos, adjust pricing, and start a low-budget visibility campaign.
  3. Week 3: Watch ratings and complaints, remove low performers from the menu, and move ads toward high-intent searches.
  4. Week 4: Review orders, AOV, rating and cost per order. Put more budget behind what is working.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do Swiggy listing changes take to show?

Most listing changes reflect within 24–48 hours. Rating improvements take two to three weeks or more of consistent operational fixes, and ranking usually follows the rating and acceptance rate.

What is a good Swiggy rating for a restaurant?

Anything above 4.2 is competitive. Below 4.0, visibility and click-through tend to fall, and below 3.8 we advise fixing operations before spending on ads.

How much should a restaurant spend on Swiggy ads?

Following the plan above (₹100–200 a day in weeks 1–2, then ₹300–500 a day in weeks 3–4), a single outlet's first month comes to roughly ₹5,600–9,800: 14 days at ₹100–200 (₹1,400–2,800) plus 14 days at ₹300–500 (₹4,200–7,000). Treat that as a ceiling to test within, not a target. Track ROAS and cost per order weekly and scale only what works.

Can I increase Swiggy orders without running ads?

Yes. Listing optimisation, menu engineering and ratings management are organic levers, and in our client work many restaurants see 15–20% order growth from these fixes alone before spending on ads (illustrative; results vary).

Does Swiggy penalise restaurants for cancellations?

In our audits, yes. High cancellation rates consistently go with weaker search ranking. Keep your acceptance rate above 95% and cancellations below 2%.

Should I copy my dine-in menu to Swiggy?

No. Build a shorter delivery menu with combos, dishes that travel well, and prices that account for commission and packaging, typically 10–15% above dine-in.
