What is menu optimization for delivery?

Menu optimization for delivery is the process of designing a short, high-conversion, high-margin menu specifically for platforms like Swiggy and Zomato.

Unlike dine-in menus, delivery menus must:

  • Maximise average order value (AOV)
  • Reduce decision fatigue
  • Highlight best-selling items
  • Be optimised for mobile browsing behaviour

Restaurants that redesign their delivery menu (instead of copying the dine-in menu) often see AOV rise by around 25–50%, along with better conversion rates and better visibility on the platforms. These figures are illustrative; results vary and are not guaranteed.

Why Most Delivery Menus Fail

Most restaurants make a critical mistake:

They upload their dine-in menu directly to Swiggy and Zomato.

This creates several problems:

  • Too many items, which confuses customers
  • Low-margin dishes, which hurt profitability
  • No bundling, which keeps cart value low
  • Poor descriptions, which lower conversion

Delivery platforms are not menu catalogues. They are conversion engines.

Menu Structure That Increases Orders

High-Converting Menu Layout

  1. Best Sellers (top section)
  2. Combos and Meals (AOV drivers)
  3. Main Items
  4. Add-Ons and Sides

Why This Works

Customers:

  • First choose a safe option (best seller)
  • Then upgrade to a combo
  • Then add extras

This increases total order value without increasing traffic.

Pricing Strategy for Delivery

Pricing must account for:

  • Platform commission (Swiggy typically 18–27% and Zomato 18–28% base commission in contracts PFC audits; press reports put the range at roughly 16–30%)
  • Customer perception
  • Competition

Recommended Pricing Approach

  • 10–20% higher than dine-in
  • Psychological pricing (₹299, ₹399)
  • Bundle pricing for value perception

Pricing Mistakes to Avoid

  • Identical dine-in pricing
  • Overpricing without value
  • No combo discounts

Frequently asked questions

How many items should a delivery menu have on Swiggy and Zomato?

As a guide, cloud kitchens work well with 15–25 items, QSRs with 20–30 and casual dining brands with 25–35. Fewer items mean faster decisions and easier operations.

Should I upload my dine-in menu to Swiggy and Zomato?

No. A dine-in menu is built for exploration, while a delivery menu has to help customers decide quickly on a phone. Build a shorter delivery menu led by best sellers and combos.

How much higher should delivery prices be than dine-in?

A common approach is 10–20% above dine-in, to account for platform commission, with psychological price points such as ₹299 or ₹399 and bundle pricing on combos.

Which menu items need photos first?

Start with your top 10 best sellers, then all combo meals, then high-margin items. Use top-down shots with consistent lighting and avoid blurry, over-edited or stock images.

Should my Swiggy and Zomato menus be identical?

Not necessarily. If performance, audience behaviour or AOV trends differ between the platforms, you can customise pricing, combos and bundles for each one.
