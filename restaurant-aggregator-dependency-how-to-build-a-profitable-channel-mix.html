A healthy restaurant business typically keeps Swiggy and Zomato at around 40–65% of delivery revenue. A profitable channel mix uses aggregators for reach and discovery while building direct ordering on WhatsApp and a website, Google Business Profile visibility, a repeat customer database and dine-in, so commission costs and ranking changes do not decide your margins.

Restaurants with excessive dependency on aggregators (80%+ of total revenue) often face margin pressure from commission costs and have limited ownership of their customers. A balanced channel mix improves long-term profitability and reduces risk.

Why Aggregator Dependency Is Increasing

Swiggy and Zomato provide:

  • strong customer reach
  • reliable logistics
  • discovery visibility

This makes them attractive growth channels. However, convenience can lead to over-dependence. Restaurants often let aggregators become their primary revenue source without building alternative acquisition channels.

The Danger of 80%+ Aggregator Dependency

Heavy dependency exposes restaurants to several risks.

Margin Pressure

Base commission is the biggest cost line. Swiggy typically charges 18–27% and Zomato 18–28% base commission in contracts PFC audits; press reports put the range at roughly 16–30% (Outlook Business). Neither platform publishes a standard rate. Our Swiggy vs Zomato commission comparison sets out these ranges and their sources in detail.

Additional cost components include:

  • discount participation
  • ad spend
  • packaging costs

Together these reduce contribution margin significantly.

Lack of Customer Ownership

Aggregator platforms control the customer relationship. Restaurants do not receive:

  • customer phone number
  • email address
  • repeat order targeting ability

This limits remarketing opportunities.

Algorithm Risk

Changes in the ranking algorithm can affect order volume. Heavy dependency increases vulnerability to ranking fluctuations.

Discount Dependency

High reliance on discounts often reduces profitability. Customers acquired through discounts may not convert into repeat buyers.

Direct Ordering: WhatsApp & Website

Direct ordering reduces commission dependency. Common direct ordering channels include:

  • website ordering system
  • WhatsApp ordering
  • QR code ordering
  • repeat customer links

Because no aggregator commission is paid, direct orders improve profit per order.

WhatsApp Ordering Strategy

WhatsApp provides a convenient ordering interface. Benefits:

  • low technology barrier
  • high customer familiarity
  • direct communication channel

Example flow: the customer opens the WhatsApp menu, the order is confirmed and delivery is arranged.

Direct orders improve contribution margin.

Website Ordering Benefits

Website ordering allows:

  • full customer ownership
  • zero commission payments
  • brand control

Website traffic can be driven through:

  • Google search
  • social media
  • repeat customers

Google Business Profile as a Channel

Google Business Profile has a big influence on discovery. Customers frequently search for “restaurant near me”, and a well-optimised profile increases visibility for those searches.

Key Optimization Areas

  • accurate opening hours
  • updated menu photos
  • customer reviews
  • correct location details

Restaurants can build credibility by delivering a consistent experience and encouraging happy customers to leave reviews.

Social Media as an Acquisition Channel

Social platforms help build brand awareness. Effective platforms include:

  • Instagram
  • Facebook

Social media supports:

  • brand recall
  • repeat customer engagement
  • direct ordering promotion

Consistent content improves long-term visibility.

Building Repeat Customer Database

Repeat customers reduce acquisition cost. Repeat order strategies include:

  • loyalty programmes
  • personalised offers
  • remarketing campaigns
  • festival promotions

Customer retention improves profitability.

Dine-In Channel Importance

Even delivery-focused restaurants benefit from a dine-in presence, because dine-in customers often become repeat delivery customers. Dine-in contributes:

  • brand trust
  • customer familiarity
  • higher ticket size

An integrated strategy improves total revenue.

Frequently asked questions

How much of my revenue should come from aggregators?

Many restaurants aim for 40–65% aggregator contribution, depending on format and location. Above 80%, margin pressure and algorithm risk become serious concerns.

Can I build direct ordering alongside Swiggy and Zomato?

Yes. Many restaurants run aggregators and direct channels such as WhatsApp, website and QR code ordering side by side, using aggregators for discovery and direct ordering for repeat customers.

What is a healthy channel mix?

A balanced mix includes aggregators, direct online orders, dine-in and repeat customers. As an indicative guide: aggregators at 40–60%, direct ordering at 15–25%, dine-in at 20–30% and repeat customers at 10–20%.

Should new restaurants rely on aggregators initially?

Aggregators often help generate early visibility, so leaning on them at launch is reasonable. The aim is to start building direct and repeat channels alongside them.

How long does it take to build direct ordering volume?

Direct channels typically grow gradually over several months, which is why the move away from heavy aggregator dependency should be step by step.
