Zomato listing optimization means improving everything a customer sees before ordering: name, cover image, cuisine tags, menu structure, dish photos, prices, delivery time and offers. The aim is to get more people who see your restaurant to click, and more of those who click to order. Because Zomato ranking rewards conversion, a stronger listing also earns more visibility.

A listing that turns more impressions into orders earns more impressions, and a weak listing loses visibility however good the food is.

The work splits into three layers:

  • The search card: what customers see while scrolling, which decides whether they click.
  • The menu page: what they see after clicking, which decides whether they order.
  • Operations: delivery time, availability and ratings, which decide whether Zomato keeps showing you.

Cover Image and Dish Photos

The cover image is the most important visual on the listing. It should show your best-selling dish, shot close and well lit, with the food filling most of the frame. A cover photo of your hero dish tells customers what you are known for in one look.

For dish photos inside the menu:

  • Photograph your top 15–20 items first. These usually account for most of your orders. Items without photos are skipped far more often.
  • Keep one consistent style. Same angle, background and lighting across the menu makes the listing look like a brand rather than a collection of uploads.
  • Show the dish as it arrives. Photos that look better than the delivered food produce disappointed customers and lower ratings.
  • Avoid text overlays and heavy filters. They make food look less real, and platforms may reject them.

Professional food photography is one of the cheapest listing upgrades relative to its effect on click-through.

Name, Cuisine Tags and Menu Structure

Cuisine Tags

Cuisine tags decide which searches and category pages you appear in. Choose the 2–4 cuisines that make up most of your sales. A North Indian restaurant that also lists Chinese, Italian and Continental dilutes its relevance for the searches it would actually win.

Menu Categories

Customers scan categories before items. Put the categories that sell most at the top, starting with a short "Bestsellers" or "Recommended" section. Keep categories to roughly 8–12 and the menu to a size the kitchen can execute consistently. Long menus slow down the decision and increase out-of-stock items.

Dish Names and Descriptions

A dish name should tell the customer what it is; a description should tell them why to choose it. Useful descriptions mention portion size or serves, key ingredients, spice level and the cooking method in one or two lines. "Serves 2, slow-cooked overnight, medium spice" does more work than "Our signature dal".

Pricing and Cost for Two

Zomato shows an approximate cost for two, and customers compare it against nearby options in the same cuisine. If your cost for two is well above the area's typical range without a visible reason (portion size, premium ingredients, a strong rating), conversion suffers.

Two rules that protect both conversion and margin:

  • Price for the channel. Delivery prices need to cover commission and packaging. Copying dine-in prices onto the app usually means losing money on every order. See menu pricing strategy for online delivery.
  • Anchor with combos. A well-built combo or meal for one gives price-sensitive customers an entry point and lifts average order value.

Delivery Time, Hours and Availability

These operational settings directly affect both ranking and conversion, and they are the most neglected part of most listings.

  • Prep time. Set it to what the kitchen actually achieves at peak. Too high pushes your delivery estimate above competitors; too low creates late orders and bad ratings.
  • Operating hours. Stay online for your full listed hours. Going offline during peaks or switching off early tells the algorithm you are unreliable.
  • Item availability. Mark items out of stock before an order comes in, not after. Rejected orders and order-level cancellations are among the most damaging signals a listing can send.

Offers and Ratings

An offer tag lifts click-through, but only some offers pay for themselves. Threshold offers and free items usually protect margin; deep percentage discounts usually do not. Run the break-even test in Swiggy and Zomato discounts: which offers make money before switching anything on.

Ratings sit above everything else. A listing with a rating below 4.0 will struggle no matter how good the photos are. Restaurants should fix the causes of low ratings, usually consistency, packaging and delivery temperature, before making cosmetic changes. See how to improve Zomato ratings.

Frequently asked questions

How long do Zomato listing changes take to show results?

Visual changes such as photos and descriptions usually go live within a few days of approval, and their effect on click-through shows within 2–3 weeks. Ranking gains take longer because they depend on the order and rating history the new listing builds.

Do photos really increase orders on Zomato?

Items with clear photos are chosen more often than items without them, and the cover image is the main driver of whether customers click your listing at all. Photograph your best sellers first.

Should my Zomato menu be the same as my dine-in menu?

No. A delivery menu should be shorter, priced to cover commission and packaging, and limited to dishes that travel well. Items that arrive soggy or cold damage ratings more than they add to sales.

How many cuisine tags should a restaurant use on Zomato?

Choose the 2–4 cuisines that make up most of your sales. Listing many unrelated cuisines dilutes your relevance for the searches you would actually win.

What should be fixed first on a Zomato listing?

Start with the search card: cover image, name, rating, cuisine tags, delivery time and offer tag. A strong menu page does nothing if customers never click, and a rating below 4.0 needs its causes fixed before cosmetic changes.
