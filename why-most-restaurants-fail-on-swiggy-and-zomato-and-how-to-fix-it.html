Why Aggregator Failure Happens Frequently

Swiggy and Zomato operate as algorithm-driven marketplaces.

Restaurants compete on:

  • Visibility
  • Conversion
  • Customer satisfaction

The platforms prioritise listings that generate:

  • More clicks
  • More orders
  • Better reviews

Restaurants that do not optimise for these signals gradually lose visibility.

Reason 1: Weak Listing That Nobody Clicks

If customers do not click your listing, orders will never happen.

Common Listing Mistakes

  • No food photos
  • Poor-quality images
  • Incomplete descriptions
  • Incorrect cuisine tags

Customers scroll quickly. Listings without strong visuals are ignored.

Fix

Improve:

  • Food photography
  • Menu descriptions
  • Category structure

Listings with optimised photos often see 2–4x higher click-through rates (illustrative; results vary and are not guaranteed).

Reason 2: Wrong Menu for Delivery

Most restaurants upload their dine-in menu directly to Swiggy and Zomato. This reduces conversion.

Problems with Dine-In Menus

  • Too many items
  • Low-margin dishes
  • No combos
  • Poor structure

Delivery customers prefer:

  • Simple choices
  • Complete meals
  • Clear pricing

Fix

Create a delivery-focused menu:

  • 20–30 items
  • Combo bundles
  • Best sellers highlighted

Restaurants optimising menu structure often increase average order value (AOV) by 25–50% (illustrative; results vary and are not guaranteed).

Reason 3: Rating Below 4.0

Ratings strongly affect ranking. Listings below 4.0 typically lose visibility.

Why Ratings Drop

Common causes:

  • Poor packaging
  • Delayed preparation
  • Wrong items delivered

Customers rarely differentiate between delivery partner issues and restaurant issues.

Fix

Ratings come from customers, so what a restaurant can control is the experience behind them. Restaurants can improve:

  • Packaging quality
  • Kitchen accuracy
  • Order preparation consistency

Ratings often recover within 3–6 weeks once these operational fixes are in place (illustrative; results vary and are not guaranteed).

Reason 4: No Ad Strategy

Organic ranking alone is rarely enough in competitive categories. Without ads, new restaurants struggle to gain traction.

Common Mistake

Either:

  • No ads at all, or
  • Excessive ad spend without optimisation

Both lead to poor return on ad spend.

Fix

Ad strategy should focus on:

  • Cost per order
  • Conversion rate
  • Visibility improvement

Balanced ad investment helps build ranking momentum.

Reason 5: Excessive Discounting That Kills Margins

Many restaurants rely heavily on discounts. Discount-driven growth is rarely sustainable.

Discount Trap

High discounts:

  • Reduce profit per order
  • Attract price-sensitive customers
  • Lower perceived brand value

Fix

Use structured offers:

  • Combo deals
  • Minimum order discounts
  • Time-based promotions

The goal is to increase order value, not just order volume.

Reason 6: Poor Operational Consistency

Algorithm ranking is influenced by operational performance.

Negative Signals

  • Order cancellations
  • Long preparation time
  • Frequent unavailability

These signals reduce platform trust.

Fix

Improve:

  • Kitchen workflow
  • Staff training
  • Inventory planning

Consistency improves ranking stability.

Reason 7: No Data Tracking or Strategy

Many restaurants rely on intuition instead of data.

Missing Metrics

Restaurants often do not track:

  • AOV
  • Conversion rate
  • Ad ROI
  • Item profitability

Without data, optimisation becomes guesswork.

Fix

Track:

  • Orders per day
  • Revenue per item
  • Ad cost per order

Data-driven decisions improve long-term profitability.

Frequently asked questions

Why do most restaurants fail on Swiggy and Zomato?

Mostly because they treat the apps as passive order channels. Weak listings, dine-in menus copied onto delivery, ratings below 4.0, heavy discounting, no ad strategy, inconsistent operations and no data tracking are the usual causes, rather than food quality alone.

How many items should a delivery menu have?

A delivery-focused menu works best with around 20–30 items, combo bundles and best sellers highlighted. Delivery customers prefer simple choices, complete meals and clear pricing.

What can a restaurant do if its rating is below 4.0?

Listings below 4.0 typically lose visibility. Restaurants can work on packaging quality, kitchen accuracy and preparation consistency, since customers rarely separate delivery partner issues from restaurant issues when they rate.

Are heavy discounts a good way to grow on Swiggy and Zomato?

Rarely. High discounts cut profit per order, attract price-sensitive customers and lower perceived brand value. Structured offers such as combo deals, minimum order discounts and time-based promotions focus on raising order value instead.

Which metrics should a restaurant track on aggregators?

At a minimum: orders per day, AOV, conversion rate, revenue per item, item profitability and ad cost per order. Without these, optimisation becomes guesswork.
