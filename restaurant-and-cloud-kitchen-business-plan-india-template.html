A restaurant business plan for India should answer one question: will each delivery order leave money after commission, discounts, packaging and food cost, and how many orders cover your fixed costs? This template covers concept, location, menu, competitors on Swiggy and Zomato, licences, capex, monthly P&L and break-even, with fill-in tables you can copy.

Most plans we see from first-time owners are pitch decks. They talk about the brand and the interiors and skip the maths. Lenders, investors and your own future self need the maths. Use the sections below as a working document, not a presentation. Fill it in, then revise it after your first 90 days of real orders.

What should a restaurant business plan in India include?

The same structure works for a dine-in restaurant and a cloud kitchen business plan. The difference is weight. A cloud kitchen plan spends more space on the aggregator plan, delivery radius and per-order economics. A dine-in plan spends more on footfall, seating and rent.

How do you size up competitors on Swiggy and Zomato?

Licences, capex and funding

Licences decide your launch date more than the kitchen fit-out does. One change matters for 2026 plans. Under the Food Safety and Standards (Licensing and Registration) Amendment Regulations, 2026, FSSAI raised its turnover limits from 1 April 2026, as summarised by S.S. Rana & Co.:

  • Basic registration: annual turnover up to ₹1.5 crore (earlier ₹12 lakh)
  • State licence: above ₹1.5 crore up to ₹50 crore (earlier ₹12 lakh to ₹20 crore)
  • Central licence: above ₹50 crore

Shop and establishment, trade or health licences and fire NOCs are state and city specific. Use our cloud kitchen licence and registration checklist to list each one with an owner and a deadline.

For capex, list every item with a written quote rather than a guess. Rent deposits, civil work and equipment vary widely by city and size, so a quote-based plan is more honest than a national average. Our cloud kitchen setup cost guide breaks down the categories. In the funding section, add at least three months of fixed costs as working capital. Aggregator payouts arrive on a cycle (NRAI described T+3 or weekly settlements in the same Storyboard18 report), while rent and salaries do not wait.

Worked example: unit economics per delivery order

This is the heart of any cloud kitchen business plan. The numbers below are illustrative only. They are not PFC client data and not a platform rate card. Replace every line with figures from your own contract and recipes.

Assumptions: menu value ₹400 per order. The restaurant funds a 10% discount. Food cost is 30% of menu value. Commission is shown at two points because rates depend on your contract. Reported ranges vary widely: Bengaluru's hotel association cited 8% to 28% in a July 2026 Free Press Journal report. GST at 18% is applied to commission and fees. Payment and other platform fees are assumed at about 2%.

Two notes on tax. Customers pay 5% GST on food, and since 2022 the aggregator collects and deposits it for delivery orders, so keep it out of your revenue line. Confirm your position with a CA before you finalise the plan.

How do you calculate break-even orders?

The formula is simple:

Break-even orders per month = Monthly fixed costs ÷ Contribution per order

Take an illustrative fixed cost of ₹3 lakh a month for rent, salaries, utilities and software. In the lower-cost case, ₹3,00,000 ÷ ₹113 is about 2,655 orders a month, or roughly 89 a day over 30 days. In the higher-cost case, ₹3,00,000 ÷ ₹49 is about 6,122 orders a month, or roughly 204 a day. The kitchen is the same. Only the deductions changed.

If your break-even needs more daily orders than the top outlets in your competitor table appear to handle, change the plan before you sign the lease. Raise average order value with combos, cut menu items with poor margins, or pick a cheaper location. Our guide to menu pricing for online delivery covers the pricing side.

Step-by-step: how to fill in this template

  1. Write the concept in one line, with a target average order value.
  2. Shortlist two or three locations and check the delivery radius in both apps.
  3. Fill the competitor table with at least ten outlets for each location.
  4. Cost every hero dish from a written recipe, including packaging.
  5. Get your commission and fee terms in writing from each platform before you model them.
  6. Build the per-order unit economics table for a lower and higher cost case.
  7. List fixed monthly costs and calculate break-even orders per day.
  8. Collect capex quotes and add three months of working capital.
  9. Map every licence with an owner and a target date.
  10. Write your top three risks, such as slow ratings, a rent increase or a key cook leaving, with a response for each.
  11. Review the plan against real payout statements after 90 days.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a cloud kitchen business plan PDF I can use?

You can turn this page into your own cloud kitchen business plan PDF. Copy the master table, the unit economics table and the P&L skeleton into a spreadsheet, fill them with your numbers, and export it. A plan filled with your own quotes and contract terms is far more useful to a bank or investor than a generic downloaded template.

What commission should I assume in my restaurant business plan?

Assume a range, not one number, until you have a signed contract. Commission depends on your agreement, city and the services you take. Recent reports cite widely different ranges, so model a lower-cost and a higher-cost case. Add GST on commission, payment fees and ads on top. The commission calculator on our site helps you test both cases quickly.

How many orders a day does a cloud kitchen need to break even?

There is no single number. Divide your monthly fixed costs by your contribution per order, then divide by days open. In our illustrative example, ₹3 lakh of fixed costs needed about 89 orders a day in a lower-cost case and about 204 in a higher-cost case. Your rent, salaries and deductions will change the answer.

Which FSSAI licence does a new restaurant need in 2026?

From 1 April 2026, FSSAI basic registration covers annual turnover up to ₹1.5 crore, a state licence covers above ₹1.5 crore up to ₹50 crore, and a central licence applies above ₹50 crore. Some activities need a central licence regardless of turnover. Check the FoSCoS portal or a compliance professional for your specific case before applying.

Should a restaurant business plan in India include both Swiggy and Zomato?

Usually yes, but plan them separately. Fees, offers, ads tools and customer mix can differ by platform and area. Build a unit economics table for each platform from its own contract terms. If one platform leaves very little contribution per order at your price point, adjust pricing or offers there rather than averaging the two together.
