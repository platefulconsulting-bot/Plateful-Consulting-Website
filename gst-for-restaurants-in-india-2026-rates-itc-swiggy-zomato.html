GST for restaurant businesses in India is simpler than it looks. Standalone restaurants, cloud kitchens and takeaway outlets charge 5% GST without input tax credit. Only restaurants inside hotels that qualify as "specified premises" charge 18% with credit. On Swiggy and Zomato orders, the platform pays that 5% to the government under Section 9(5), not you.

Not tax advice. This guide explains the rules as we understand them on 12 November 2026. GST notifications change often, and your position depends on your registration, premises and turnover. Confirm every decision with a chartered accountant before you file a return or change your billing.

What is the GST for restaurant services in 2026?

GST law treats food served by a restaurant, eating joint, mess, canteen or cloud kitchen as "restaurant service". It does not matter whether the customer eats at your table, picks up a parcel or gets it delivered. A CBIC circular from October 2021 confirmed that cloud kitchens and central kitchens fall under restaurant service. So the restaurant GST rate depends on where you operate, not on the order type.

Did GST 2.0 in September 2025 change restaurant GST?

Not the headline rate. The 56th GST Council meeting brought the "GST 2.0" rate rationalisation, and CBIC's Notification 15/2025-Central Tax (Rate) dated 17 September 2025 applied the services changes from 22 September 2025. Restaurant service stayed at 5% without ITC, and at 18% with ITC at specified premises. Three things did change for food businesses:

  • Hotel rooms up to ₹7,500: now 5% without ITC instead of 12%. That matters only if you run rooms as well as a kitchen.
  • Standalone restaurants cannot choose 18%: the same notification added an explanation, effective from 1 April 2025, that "premises" means a place from where hotel accommodation is supplied. A restaurant with no rooms cannot declare itself specified premises to charge 18% and claim credit.
  • Delivery fees on apps: from 22 September 2025, platforms became liable for 18% GST on local delivery where the delivery partner is not registered, as Inc42 reported in September 2025. This sits on the customer's delivery fee, not on your food bill. The rate changed again in November 2026 (see below).

Two more changes followed from 1 November 2026, under Notification 06/2026-Central Tax (Rate) dated 27 October 2026. First, it replaced the delivery entry (serial 12, item (iii)). Delivery services supplied through an e-commerce operator by a person not liable to register, and delivery of goods supplied through an operator, now carry 5% GST on condition that no ITC is taken, down from 18% with ITC. Commentary on the notification says the platform pays this under Section 9(5). Second, a 5% restaurant can now take credit on one thing: restaurant service it procures from another restaurant service provider (the "same line of business"). GST on rent, equipment, packaging, marketing and platform commission is still not creditable. Check with your CA before claiming it.

Input tax credit: what can a restaurant actually claim?

If you charge 5%, assume you claim almost nothing. The GST you pay on kitchen equipment, rent, packaging, software and app commission becomes part of your cost. Swiggy and Zomato charge GST on their commission and fees, and a 5% restaurant cannot set that off. When you work out what an app order really earns, add that GST to the commission. If you operate at specified premises and charge 18%, normal ITC rules apply. You can claim credit on eligible inputs, subject to the usual conditions such as valid invoices and supplier filings.

Composition scheme for restaurants: who is eligible?

The composition scheme lets small restaurants pay a flat 5% of turnover (2.5% CGST plus 2.5% SGST) with simpler returns. Under Notification 14/2019-Central Tax, you can opt in if your aggregate turnover in the preceding financial year was up to ₹1.5 crore. The limit is ₹75 lakh in Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim, Tripura and Uttarakhand.

GST registration for restaurant: when is it mandatory?

Restaurant service is a supply of services, so the registration threshold is ₹20 lakh of aggregate turnover in a year in most states, and ₹10 lakh in a few special category states. Two points trip up new owners:

  • App sales count. CBIC Circular 167/23/2021-GST says orders supplied through Swiggy and Zomato are included in your aggregate turnover for the threshold.
  • Unregistered does not mean untaxed. The same circular says the platform must pay GST on restaurant service supplied through it, even by an unregistered person.

Many owners register voluntarily from day one. The apps' own document lists change, so check our Swiggy registration guide and the cloud kitchen licence checklist before you apply.

How does GST on Swiggy Zomato orders work?

Since 1 January 2022, restaurant service ordered through an e-commerce operator is notified under Section 9(5) of the CGST Act. The platform is treated as the supplier for GST purposes. It collects 5% GST from the customer, pays it to the government in cash, and issues the invoice for the restaurant service. Circular 167 also says the platform no longer collects TCS on restaurant orders where it pays tax under Section 9(5). The exception is restaurants at specified premises, which are outside this rule and pay GST themselves.

Do not pay GST twice on the same app order. A common mistake is billing software that adds GST to aggregator sales and pushes them into normal taxable supplies. Your app sales in your returns should match the e-commerce figures the platform reports.

Where GST shows up on your payout statement

Your weekly settlement usually shows GST in several places: the GST the platform collected on food (which it pays to the government, not to you), GST charged on commission and fees, any TCS on non-restaurant supplies, and income-tax TDS on e-commerce payouts. GST checklist for restaurants and cloud kitchens

  1. Add up your aggregate turnover, including all Swiggy and Zomato sales.
  2. Decide on registration. Above ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh in some states) it is compulsory; below that it is optional.
  3. If eligible, compare the regular scheme and composition with your CA, based on your channel mix.
  4. Confirm whether you are at specified premises. If you run no hotel rooms, you are not.
  5. Set your POS to charge 5% GST only on direct orders, shown separately on the bill (or issue a bill of supply under composition).
  6. Keep aggregator orders out of your own tax liability and report them in the e-commerce tables every month.
  7. Reconcile app sales in your books with platform reports and payout statements monthly.
  8. Treat GST on commission, rent and packaging as a cost in your menu pricing, not as a credit.
  9. Review your setup with your CA whenever a new rate notification comes out, as happened in September 2025 and November 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Is GST on restaurant food 5% or 18%?

For almost every standalone restaurant, café, QSR and cloud kitchen in India, GST on restaurant food is 5% without input tax credit. That applies to dine-in, takeaway and delivery. The 18% rate with credit applies only to restaurants at specified premises, meaning hotels that supplied any room unit above ₹7,500 per day in the previous financial year or filed an opt-in declaration. A restaurant with no rooms cannot opt for 18%.

Do I pay GST on Swiggy and Zomato orders?

Not on the food, if you are a standalone restaurant or cloud kitchen. Since 1 January 2022, Swiggy and Zomato pay 5% GST on restaurant service ordered through them under Section 9(5) of the CGST Act, and issue the invoice to the customer. You still report these sales in your GST returns as supplies through an e-commerce operator. You also pay GST on the platform's commission and fees, which you cannot claim back.

Do I need GST registration to sell on Swiggy and Zomato?

Under GST law, restaurant service needs registration once aggregate turnover crosses ₹20 lakh a year in most states, or ₹10 lakh in a few special category states. Your app sales count towards that limit. Below the limit, registration is optional, and the platform pays GST on your app orders either way. The apps set their own onboarding document requirements, so check the current list before applying.

Can a restaurant claim input tax credit under GST?

A restaurant charging 5% cannot claim input tax credit on rent, equipment, raw material, packaging or platform commission. From 1 November 2026, Notification 06/2026 allows credit only on restaurant service procured from another restaurant service provider. Restaurants at specified premises charging 18% can claim normal input tax credit. Because credit is blocked for most outlets, GST on your costs should be built into your menu pricing.

Can a restaurant on the composition scheme sell on food delivery apps?

A restaurant with up to ₹1.5 crore turnover in the previous year (₹75 lakh in some north-eastern and hill states) can opt for composition and pay 5% of turnover, but cannot collect GST from customers or claim credit. Restaurants serving alcohol cannot opt in. If you plan to sell heavily on Swiggy and Zomato under composition, confirm with your CA how those app sales are reported before choosing.
