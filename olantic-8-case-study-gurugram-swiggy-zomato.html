Olantic 8, a new Tandoor and Chinese delivery kitchen in Gurugram, grew combined Swiggy and Zomato sales from ₹28.2K in its first full month live to ₹3.65L in month seven, a 12.9x jump. Orders grew 10.8x, from 98 to 1,058 a month, while the average bill stayed flat. This is how Plateful Consulting (PFC) built that growth.

Where Olantic 8 started

Olantic 8 is a delivery-first kitchen in Gurugram serving Indian tandoor dishes and Chinese. The owner came to PFC before the brand was live on either platform, so there was no earlier online sales history to compare against.

That matters for how you read this case study. This is a launch-growth story for a new listing, not a before-PFC versus after-PFC comparison. Every number below starts from the first full month the kitchen was live online. All figures come from PFC's monthly reports, compiled from the Swiggy and Zomato partner dashboards.

Launching on both platforms

PFC handled the launch on Swiggy and Zomato end to end: onboarding, document collection and submission, correcting the outlet address so the kitchen showed up for the right delivery radius, and thumbnails for the listing. A new kitchen loses weeks when an address pin is wrong or a document is rejected, so getting this right first time was the base for everything that followed.

A menu built for delivery

Next, PFC rebuilt the menu for how people order on apps, not how they order at a counter. That covered both the Indian section and a full Chinese menu:

  • Pricing set for delivery economics, including a ₹10 price correction on selected categories to protect margin.
  • Half and full portions so solo diners and families could both find a fit.
  • Add-ons on the dishes where customers naturally want extras.
  • Photos and descriptions on the items that carry the menu, so customers know what they are getting before they order.

The average order value was ₹288 in the first month and then held in a narrow band of roughly ₹330–355 for the rest of the period. In other words, the growth did not come from pushing bigger bills. It came from more people ordering, month on month.

Ads that scaled only when they paid back

PFC's rule on ads was simple: increase the budget step by step, and only while the spend keeps paying back. ROI figures here are as reported by the platforms' own ad dashboards.

Swiggy

Monthly Swiggy ad spend grew from ₹4.6K to ₹40.6K over the period. Over the same stretch, platform-reported ROI rose from 4.6x to 8.9x. Spend went up nearly nine times and returns per rupee still improved, because budget was only added where the data supported it.

Zomato

Zomato started very small. An early spend of just ₹398 showed a 9.3x ROI. As the budget scaled about 92x to ₹36.7K a month, ROI came down, which is normal when you move from a tiny test to real reach. It still returned 4.8x in the latest month. A falling ROI on a much larger base is not a problem as long as it stays well above break-even.

Discounts without giving away margin

New brands often switch on a blanket "flat 50% off" and then struggle to turn it off. PFC took a narrower approach:

  1. 70:30 discount sharing with Swiggy. PFC negotiated a structure where Swiggy funds 30% of the discount, so the kitchen was not carrying the full cost.
  2. ₹99 Store items to get price-sensitive first-time customers in the door.
  3. Freebie on a minimum order to lift basket size without cutting item prices.
  4. Flash-sale items for short, targeted pushes instead of a permanent markdown.

Kitchen discipline that protected growth

Growth on delivery apps breaks down quickly if the kitchen cannot keep up. PFC ran a monthly review with the owner covering prep time, online availability, complaints and lost sales. The results:

  • Zomato kitchen prep time came down from 26 minutes to 11 minutes.
  • Swiggy lost sales fell from ₹1,564 to ₹0.
  • Online availability stayed above 99% on both platforms in the later months.

Growth was not a straight line. As volume rose, complaints rose with it, and the next two months were softer: growth slowed in month four and sales dipped in month five. The monthly reviews pinpointed the operational fixes, and the two months that followed were the strongest of the period.

PFC also launched additional brand storefronts, Chinese Chilli & Wok and Spice & Wok, from the same kitchen. Each storefront captures search demand that a single brand name cannot, without adding rent or staff.

What other new delivery brands can take from this

  • Get the launch basics right. Documents, address pin and thumbnails decide how quickly you start getting orders.
  • Build the menu for the app. Portions, add-ons, photos and delivery-aware pricing do more than a long item list.
  • Scale ads on evidence. Start small, add budget only while returns hold, and judge ROI against the size of the spend.
  • Use targeted offers, not blanket ones. Shared-funding deals, ₹99 items and freebies protect margin better than flat discounts.
  • Treat operations as a growth lever. Prep time, availability and lost sales should be reviewed every month, especially when volume jumps.
  • Expect a dip. A soft patch after fast growth is common. Fixing the cause is what sets up the next leg.

Results vary; this is one client's outcome, not a guarantee.
