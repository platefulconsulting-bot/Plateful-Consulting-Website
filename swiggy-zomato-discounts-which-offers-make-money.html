Only some of them do. A Swiggy or Zomato discount pays for itself only when the extra orders it brings in are worth more than the margin it gives away on every order, including the orders you would have got anyway. Threshold offers and free items usually pass this test; deep percentage offers usually fail it.

Run that test on the illustrative numbers in this article and the results split cleanly:

  • Threshold offers (flat ₹ off above a minimum order) and free items usually need fewer than 10% more orders to break even.
  • Uncapped or high-cap percentage offers (40–60% off) often need 70–110% more orders just to stand still.

Most restaurants losing money on aggregators are not losing it to commission alone. They are losing it to offers that were never checked against this test.

The Break-Even Test to Run Before Any Offer Goes Live

Before switching an offer on, calculate how many more orders it needs just to keep your total contribution where it is today:

Required order lift = (contribution per order without the offer ÷ contribution per order with it) − 1

When a Discount Is Worth Running

Discounts are a tool for specific jobs, not something to leave running in the background. They earn their cost in four situations:

  • A new listing with no order history. The algorithm has nothing to rank you on. A time-boxed launch offer of 3–4 weeks helps build the order volume and early ratings that organic visibility depends on.
  • Slow dayparts. An offer limited to 3–6 pm fills kitchen capacity you are already paying for, so the extra orders cost you food and packaging but no extra fixed cost.
  • Bringing back lapsed customers. Offers aimed at customers who have not ordered in 30–60 days cost less than winning new ones.
  • Launching a dish. An item-level discount gets trial on a new product without discounting the whole menu.

Outside these four situations, a permanent offer is a price cut that you never decided to make. Customers start treating the discounted price as the real price, and taking the offer away then hurts conversion.

Signs Your Offers Are Burning Margin

Check these every month from your partner dashboard:

  • Share of orders using an offer is above 70%. Your full-price customer base is shrinking.
  • Average order value is sitting at or just below your threshold. The threshold is too low and is discounting orders that would have happened anyway.
  • Repeat rate falls after an offer ends. The offer bought one-off orders, not loyal customers.
  • Orders rise but net payout per week does not. This is the break-even test failing in real life.

If two or more of these are true, cut the offer depth before you add ad spend. Advertising a loss-making offer only brings in more loss-making orders. Our guide to Swiggy ads strategy covers how offers and ads should work together.

How Discounts Affect Your Ranking

Offers lift click-through and conversion, and both platforms reward those signals with visibility. That is why a discount often produces a quick ranking bump.

The bump is borrowed. When the offer ends, conversion drops back and the ranking follows. Lasting visibility comes from signals a discount cannot buy and that restaurants build through their own operations: ratings, prep-time accuracy, low rejection rates and menu quality. These are explained in detail in how the Swiggy ranking algorithm works.

Use offers to start that cycle on a new or recovering listing, then pull back as the operational signals take over.

  1. Week 1: Audit. List every live offer on both platforms. For each one, record the share of orders using it and calculate its break-even lift.
  2. Week 2: Replace. Turn off any always-on high-cap percentage offer. Replace it with a threshold offer set about 20% above your current AOV, or a free item with a low food cost.

Frequently asked questions

Who pays for discounts on Swiggy and Zomato?

It depends on the offer. Restaurant-funded offers come entirely out of your payout. Platform-funded and co-funded campaigns split the cost, so check the funding share in your partner dashboard before opting in.

Is commission charged before or after the discount?

On most restaurant-funded offers, commission is calculated on the order value after the discount, which slightly softens the hit. The exact base is set in your agreement, so confirm it against a real payout statement rather than assuming.

Should I stop all discounts on Swiggy and Zomato?

No. Stop the ones that fail the break-even test. Threshold offers, free items and time-boxed launch or daypart offers usually protect margin; always-on deep percentage discounts usually do not.

How do I work out whether an offer will make money?

Divide your contribution per order without the offer by your contribution per order with it, then subtract one. The result is the order lift the offer needs just to break even. If you are unlikely to see that lift sustained, the offer will cost you money.

Where should I set the minimum order for a threshold offer?

About 15–25% above your current average order value. Set it higher and few customers reach it; set it lower and you end up discounting orders that were already that size.
