Zomato's base commission is typically 18–28% of order value in the contracts we audit, and press reports put the market range at about 16–30%. On top of that come 18% GST on commission, a payment mechanism fee, ads, discounts and, on longer deliveries, a distance fee. Together these usually take about 25–40% of each order.

Zomato does not publish a rate card. It sets the commission, which its partner terms now call a "service fee", per restaurant in each partner's enrolment form (Zomato partner terms). The rate you pay depends on your category, city and the terms you agreed when you signed up.

Many restaurant owners focus only on the base commission percentage, but the real profitability impact comes from the combined cost of commission, fees, ads, discounts and taxes. Understanding this full breakdown is essential for any restaurant operating on Zomato.

Why Understanding Zomato Commission Matters

Food delivery platforms operate on a commission-based marketplace model. Zomato connects restaurants with customers, handles order routing and provides delivery. In exchange, restaurants pay a percentage of each completed order.

The issue is that commission is charged on gross revenue, not profit.

Example (illustrative):

MetricExample order
Order value₹400
Base commission (25%)₹100
GST on commission (18%)₹18
Payment mechanism fee (assumed 2%)₹8
GST on payment fee (18%)₹1.44
Net payout before food cost₹272.56

The maths: ₹400 − ₹100 − ₹18 − ₹8 − ₹1.44 = ₹272.56. If food cost for the order is ₹140, the restaurant is left with about ₹132.56 before rent, staff and operations.

We have assumed a 2% payment mechanism fee on the order value for simplicity. Your actual percentage is set in your contract, and Zomato's terms calculate it on order value including taxes, so the rupee amount can be slightly higher. This is why many restaurants underestimate the real impact of delivery platform charges.

GST, Payment Fees and Other Charges

Beyond the base commission, several financial components affect what you keep.

GST on Commission

Restaurants pay 18% GST on the commission, because Zomato's fees are exclusive of applicable taxes. If commission is ₹100, GST adds ₹18. This tax applies to the commission amount, not the full order value.

The 5% GST on the food itself is different: it has been collected by the platform from the customer and paid to the government since 1 January 2022, so it is not a cost to you.

Payment Mechanism Fee

Zomato's partner terms list a separate payment mechanism fee, charged as a percentage of order value including taxes, so check this line on every payout statement. It is a standard charge, not an occasional one, and GST applies to it as well. In our examples we assume 2%; use the rate in your own contract.

Long-Distance Fee

Orders delivered beyond a set distance carry an extra restaurant-side fee, introduced in 2025. Because the amount has been reported differently and can change, read it from your partner app rather than relying on a published figure. Note that Zomato can revise fee rates on 7 days' notice under its partner terms.

Promotional Discount Funding

Zomato frequently promotes discounts such as:

  • Flat ₹100 off

The funding for these offers may be split between Zomato and the restaurant. Zomato's terms say restaurant-funded promotions apply only where the restaurant has agreed to them (Zomato partner terms). Strategies to Maintain Profit Despite Commission

High commission does not automatically mean delivery is unprofitable. Restaurants that do well on Zomato typically apply several operational strategies.

1. Delivery-Specific Menu Pricing

In our experience, delivery menu prices are often 10–20% higher than dine-in prices to offset platform charges. Customers expect some difference on aggregator apps. See our menu pricing guide for delivery.

2. Combo Bundles to Increase AOV

Higher cart value improves profitability.

Example:

ItemPrice
Single dish₹240
Meal combo₹420

Even with the same order count, higher AOV improves margins, because several fixed per-order costs, such as packaging and any per-order fees, are spread over a bigger basket.

3. Packaging Cost Recovery

Zomato's terms allow restaurants to charge customers for food, beverages and packaging. A sensible packaging charge helps offset container and packing costs.

4. Commission Review

Restaurants with growing order volume should review their commission terms every 6–12 months with their account manager. Most restaurants never revisit their initial agreement.

What does a restaurant actually pay Zomato per order?
