Mohan Sweets, a sweets and snacks shop in Palam/Dabri, Dwarka, grew its monthly Swiggy and Zomato sales from ₹23.2K in the month before PFC to ₹6.71 lakh in its fifth month with us, roughly 29x. The order of work mattered: availability, menu and kitchen speed were fixed first, and ads were scaled only as returns came in.

The starting point: a busy offline shop with almost no online sales

Mohan Sweets is a neighbourhood sweet shop in Palam/Dabri, Dwarka, New Delhi. Delivery apps were an afterthought. In the month before PFC, the outlet did ₹23,155 in online sales across both apps, and almost all of it came from Zomato:

  • Zomato: ₹21,988 from 84 orders.
  • Swiggy: ₹1,167, effectively a dormant listing.
  • Zomato online availability: 77.9%, so the outlet was offline for a large share of the hours customers could have ordered.
  • Zomato ad ROI: 1.2x.

At onboarding, a target of ₹5–6 lakh in monthly online sales was set, within a six-month plan.

What PFC did: fix the basics before ads

The work followed a clear sequence. Ads can only amplify what a listing already does, so the foundations came first.

  1. Availability fixed first. Zomato online availability went from 77.9% in the month before PFC to 99%+ by the second and third months. The outlet stopped losing orders to downtime, which made this the cheapest growth lever in the engagement.
  2. Swiggy launched from near zero. Swiggy moved from ₹1.2K in the month before PFC to ₹12.4K in the onboarding month and ₹2.23 lakh by the fifth month.
  3. Menu rebuilt for delivery. The full sweets and snacks menu was set up on both apps with photos, pricing and variants. Our Zomato listing optimisation guide walks through the same checklist.
  4. Kitchen speed held. Zomato prep time stayed at 2–4 minutes through the growth months, even as monthly orders climbed past 2,000.
  5. Festive menu pushes. Festive offerings were pushed for Raksha Bandhan, when sweets demand peaks.
  6. Ads scaled on returns. Budgets were increased as ad returns held up, covered in the next section.

Over the same stretch, the Swiggy rating moved from 3.5 in the onboarding month to 4.6 in the fourth month. Ratings come from customer reviews, and we report this as context rather than as a PFC action.

Discount discipline when margin became the priority

Volume is not the only goal. Later in the engagement, the owners said they wanted margin over volume on sweets. PFC removed the deep discount codes and moved the outlet to flat discounts the owners approved. Running an account to the owner's priorities includes pulling back on discounting when the owner asks.

The takeaway for Delhi sweet shops and restaurants

  • Check availability before anything else. If your outlet is offline for a fifth of the day, fix that before you spend on ads.
  • Be live on both apps. Swiggy went from almost nothing to ₹2.23 lakh a month for Mohan Sweets.
  • Make the online menu match the counter. Photos, variants and correct pricing help customers find and order what you sell.
  • Use festivals. Push festive offerings when sweets demand peaks.
  • Scale ads on returns. Increase budgets as ROI holds, not on a fixed schedule.
  • Agree the discount policy with the owner. Flat, approved discounts can protect margin.

Frequently asked questions

How much did Mohan Sweets' online sales grow with PFC?

Monthly Swiggy and Zomato sales went from ₹23,155 in the month before PFC to about ₹6.71 lakh in the fifth month, roughly 29x. The ₹5–6 lakh monthly target set at onboarding was crossed in the fifth month, inside the six-month plan.

Why fix availability before running ads?

Ads send customers to your listing. If the outlet is offline, that spend is wasted. Mohan Sweets' Zomato availability went from 77.9% to 99%+ in the early months, and Zomato ad ROI rose from 1.2x before PFC to 6.0x by the third month.

Is it worth launching Swiggy if most of my orders come from Zomato?

For Mohan Sweets it was. Swiggy went from ₹1,167 in the month before PFC to ₹2.23 lakh in the fifth month, giving the shop a second strong channel instead of relying on one app.

Did PFC rely on heavy discounts for this growth?

When the owners asked to prioritise margin, deep discount codes were removed and replaced with flat, approved discounts. The discount policy is set with the owner, not imposed on them.

Why is one month missing from the data?

The fourth month's report is not available to us, so that month isn't shown rather than estimated. All other figures come from PFC's monthly reports, based on the Swiggy and Zomato partner dashboards.
