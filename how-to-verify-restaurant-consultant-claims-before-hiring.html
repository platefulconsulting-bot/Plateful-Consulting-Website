To verify a restaurant consultant's claims before hiring, ask for named case studies with dated before-and-after dashboard screenshots, call two or three past clients, check verified reviews, and confirm whether press coverage is paid. Then read the contract for lock-in, exit and reporting terms. Promises of guaranteed results or secret commission rates are the clearest warning signs.

Why should you verify a restaurant consultant's claims?

Anyone can post a screenshot of a big number. Few show the month before it, the ad spend behind it or the discount that paid for it. When you hire someone for Swiggy and Zomato growth, you pay a fee and you hand over access to the accounts that carry your sales. A poor hire can push you into heavy discounts, waste ad budget or damage ratings.

Start by deciding what kind of help you need. A consultant, an agency and the platform's own account manager do different jobs, and they get paid in different ways. Our comparison of consultants, agencies and platform account managers sets out who does what.

What should a real case study show?

A useful case study answers six questions. Which outlet, in which city? Which platform? Which metric: net sales, GMV, orders or payout? Which month is the "before", and is it a clean baseline? How long is the window? What happened to ad spend and discounts over the same period?

Without these, a growth multiple means little. One of our delivery clients went from about ₹23,200 to ₹6.71 lakh a month in online sales, a 29x jump from a tiny base. A dine-in client went from ₹33.81 lakh to ₹51.32 lakh a month, or +51.8%. The second is a smaller multiple, but it can be the harder result. Ask to see both kinds.

Also check whether growth was bought. On our own results page, one Noida outlet's Swiggy net order value went from ₹5.23 lakh to ₹18.00 lakh a month. Discount spend also rose, from ₹2.28 lakh to ₹14.44 lakh, and we show both numbers. Any consultant should show you this without being asked.

Honest data comes with caveats. Our 2026 benchmarks from PFC client data cover 12 case studies. The median growth was 6.5x for small-base outlets and about 1.7x for established ones. The page also says plainly that the sample is not random and leans towards cases that went well. Expect that disclosure from anyone you pay.

How do you check references properly?

Ask for two or three owners you can call directly. Choose at least one with a similar format to yours, such as a cloud kitchen, a QSR or a fine-dine outlet. If you can, ask for one client who has since stopped working with the consultant.

On the call, ask what changed in their numbers, how long it took, what they spent on ads and discounts, how often they got reports, and whether they would hire the consultant again. Listen for specifics: a month, a metric and what caused the change.

What do online reviews and the Google Business Profile tell you?

Look up the consultant's Google Business Profile. Check that the address and phone number match what they gave you. Real reviews are spread across months and mention specific work. A burst of short five-star reviews in one week is worth a question.

Google treats fake reviews seriously. Its Business Profile help page says that, beyond removing fake reviews, it can block new reviews for a period, unpublish existing ones and show customers a warning notice. In India, the BIS standard IS 19000:2022 on online consumer reviews covers food delivery platforms. As Business Today reported, it bars publishing reviews that were bought or written by people employed for that purpose. If a consultant offers to buy reviews for your restaurant, assume they bought their own.

Is that press coverage editorial or paid?

Many "as featured in" logos come from paid press releases. Paid PR is legal, but it is not independent proof.

Open the actual article and scroll to the end. Syndicated releases often carry a line such as "ADVERTORIAL DISCLAIMER: The above press release has been provided by VMPL", followed by a note that the news agency is not responsible for the content. Labels such as "Sponsored" or "Partner content" are other signs. Newslaundry has explained how such releases end up on news sites looking like reporting. In August 2025, ASCI added a clause asking media companies to label paid posts on their social media handles.

A word on commission. What you pay Swiggy and Zomato is set in your agreement with each platform. It varies by contract, city and the services you use, so it is a range, not a secret code someone can unlock. See our Swiggy vs Zomato commission comparison for 2026 for how it works.

Who should own the dashboard access?

You should. The partner accounts, the registered phone number, the email and the bank details must stay in your name. A consultant needs working access, not ownership. When the work ends, change passwords and remove access the same day. Ask for monthly report exports so your history stays with you.

What should the contract and pricing cover?

The contract should state the scope of work, the metrics you will track, the baseline month, the reporting cadence (weekly, monthly or both), the minimum term, the notice period and what you get at exit. It should also say whether ad spend and discount funding are included in the fee. Usually they are not.

A 10-step checklist before you hire

  1. Decide whether you need a consultant, an agency or more support from your platform account manager.
  2. Ask for three named case studies with dated before-and-after dashboard screenshots.
  3. Check each case study for the metric, baseline month, window, ad spend and discount spend.
  4. Call two or three references yourself, including one past client if possible.
  5. Read the consultant's Google Business Profile reviews for spread over time and real detail.
  6. Open every "featured in" article and look for sponsored or advertorial labels.
  7. Reject anyone offering guaranteed multiples, review buying or special commission IDs.
  8. Confirm you keep ownership of all partner accounts, contact details and bank details.
  9. Get a written contract covering scope, reporting cadence, lock-in, notice and exit handover.
  10. Agree on a baseline and the metrics you will review together after the first 60 to 90 days.

Frequently asked questions

How many references should I ask a restaurant consultant for?

Ask for two or three, and call them yourself without the consultant on the line. Try to include one outlet that is similar to yours in format and city, and one client who has stopped working with them. Ask what changed in orders and net sales, what they spent on ads and discounts, how often they got reports, and whether they would hire the consultant again.

Are before-and-after screenshots enough proof?

Not on their own. A screenshot should show the outlet name, the platform, the dates and the metric. Ask for the month before the work started and the month being compared, plus ad spend and discount spend for both. A growth multiple from a very small base can look dramatic, so ask what the starting number was before you judge the result.

Can a consultant get me a lower Swiggy or Zomato commission?

Commission is set in your agreement with each platform and varies by contract, so treat any "special low commission ID" or hidden rate as a red flag. A consultant can help you understand your contract and protect margin through legit levers. These include menu pricing for delivery, better discount design, tighter ad spend and fewer cancellations and lost orders.

How do I know if press coverage of a consultant is paid?

Open the article and check the top and bottom of the page. Paid releases often carry an advertorial disclaimer naming the content provider, such as VMPL, along with a note that the publisher is not responsible for the content. Labels like "Sponsored" or "Partner content" mean the same thing. Paid PR is fine, but it is not independent proof of results.

Should I give my consultant my Swiggy and Zomato password?

Keep the account, the registered phone number, the email and the bank details in your own name. Give the consultant only the access they need to run menus, offers and ads, and record who has it. When the engagement ends, change passwords and remove access the same day. Ask for regular report exports so your sales history stays with you, not with them.
