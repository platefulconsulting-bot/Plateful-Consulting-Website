What is a cloud kitchen franchise and how does it work?

A cloud kitchen franchise lets you run a delivery-only kitchen under someone else's brand. You get the name, recipes, menu, and usually a supply chain and listing support. In return you pay an upfront fee, an ongoing royalty, and often other charges. If you are new to the format, read what a cloud kitchen is and the main models in India first.

Cloud kitchen franchises in India usually fall into one of these structures. The names vary by franchisor, so ask which one you are signing.

Kitchen-in-kitchen is the lowest-risk version for an existing restaurant. You already have the space, staff, and licences. The question is only whether the extra brand earns more than the royalty and the attention it takes from your main menu.

What does a cloud kitchen franchise cost?

Cloud kitchen franchise cost has two layers: what you pay the franchisor, and what you spend to build and run the kitchen. Published figures vary a lot between brands and change often, and most numbers online come from franchise listing sites rather than the brands themselves. So treat any figure you see as a starting point for questions, not a quote.

Neither is always better. The trade-off is speed and a ready menu against control and margin.

One point people miss: a franchise does not run your kitchen for you. Prep times, packaging, stock-outs, and ratings still depend on your team. A strong brand with a slow kitchen will drop in ranking like any other listing.

What should you check in the franchise agreement?

India has no dedicated franchise law. There is no mandatory disclosure document, cooling-off period, or registration of franchise deals. The agreement is the main source of your rights, as the law firm S.S. Rana & Co. notes, with general laws like the Indian Contract Act and Trade Marks Act in the background. That makes the contract important. Get a lawyer to read it, and check these clauses yourself:

  • Royalty base. Gross order value, net sales after discounts, or net payout after commission. The difference can be large on Swiggy and Zomato. Learn to read your payout statement before you agree to a base.
  • Minimum royalty. A fixed monthly minimum means you pay even in a bad month.
  • Who owns the Swiggy and Zomato listing. Whose name, PAN, GST, and bank account sit on the platform account, and who can log in to the dashboard.
  • Supply lock-in. Which items you must buy from the franchisor and whether prices are fixed or can be revised anytime.
  • Discount control. Whether the franchisor can push you into platform offers that you fund.
  • Territory. Whether another franchisee of the same brand can open in your delivery radius.
  • Term, renewal, and exit. Lock-in period, renewal fee, termination grounds, refund of deposit, and what happens to your equipment.
  • Non-compete. How long and how wide. Indian courts often strike down broad post-term non-competes, but a fight costs money.
  • Licences. The kitchen usually needs its own FSSAI licence and GST registration. Check who applies and in whose name, using our cloud kitchen licence checklist.

Red flags that should make you walk away

  • Guaranteed or assured monthly returns. No one can promise Swiggy or Zomato orders. In June 2026, Delhi Police's Economic Offences Wing arrested three directors over a food franchise scheme that allegedly promised assured monthly returns to investors, as The Tribune reported.
  • Order claims you cannot verify. "Our outlets do 150 orders a day" with no dashboard screenshots, no outlet names, and no franchisee contacts.
  • Referral or recruitment income. If you earn by bringing in other franchisees, it is not a food business.
  • Forced supplies at high markups. Mandatory purchase of everyday items like oil, rice, or plain packaging at above-market prices.
  • Pressure to pay fast. "Only two cities left" and a discount if you pay this week.
  • No kitchen audit or location check. A franchisor who does not care where you open is earning from fees, not from your sales.
  • Vague agreements. Fees described in a brochure but missing from the contract.

Due-diligence checklist before you sign

  1. Search the brand on Swiggy and Zomato from your own kitchen location. Note whether it shows up, its rating, its reviews, and how many outlets deliver to your area already.
  2. Ask for live Swiggy and Zomato partner dashboards of at least three existing franchisees, shown on screen, not as PDFs. Look at orders, average order value, rating, and ad spend over six months.
  3. Talk to three franchisees on your own, including at least one the franchisor did not suggest. Find them through the app listings. Ask what they earn after all fees, and whether they would sign again.
  4. Get the full fee sheet in writing, including GST, supply price list, and any minimums.
  5. Build your own monthly P&L. Start from realistic orders, deduct platform commission, discounts, ads, royalty, marketing fee, food cost, rent, staff, and utilities. Our business plan template helps.
  6. Check the company on the MCA portal: directors, incorporation date, and whether it is active.
  7. Search the brand and directors' names with words like "fraud", "FIR", and "complaint".
  8. Have a lawyer review the agreement, especially royalty base, exit, and non-compete.
  9. Confirm who will own the Swiggy and Zomato accounts and whether you get full dashboard access.
  10. Only then compare it with launching your own brand from the same kitchen.

How to judge the best cloud kitchen franchise for your area

There is no single best cloud kitchen franchise in India. A biryani brand that leads in one city may be invisible three kilometres from your kitchen. Judge it locally. Does the brand already rank on the first scroll for its category in your area? Is its rating holding up across outlets? Does the menu suit your local average order value? And are there already two outlets of the same brand delivering to your customers? Saturation is common with fast-growing franchisors.

Also look at fit with your setup. If you already run a restaurant, a kitchen-in-kitchen brand that shares your ingredients can add orders without new rent. If you are starting fresh, a franchise only makes sense if its demand in your radius is proven, because you carry the full setup cost either way.

Frequently asked questions

Is a cloud kitchen franchise profitable in India?

It can be, but profit depends on local demand, your kitchen's operations, and the total fees. Royalty and marketing fees come on top of Swiggy and Zomato commission, discounts, and ads. Build a monthly P&L from realistic orders before signing. If the numbers only work at the order levels in the brochure, treat it as a warning sign rather than a plan.

How much does a cloud kitchen franchise cost in India?

There is no standard figure. You pay the franchisor a one-time fee, an ongoing royalty, often a marketing fee, and a margin on mandatory supplies. You also fund rent, equipment, licences, staff, and working capital, which vary by city. Get every charge in writing, including GST, and compare it with your own estimated setup cost.

Do I need my own FSSAI licence and GST for a franchise kitchen?

Usually, yes. The kitchen premises needs its own FSSAI licence or registration, and the business selling on Swiggy and Zomato generally needs GST registration, PAN, and a bank account. Some franchisors handle the paperwork, but check in whose name each licence and platform account is issued. That decides who controls the listing if you exit.

Is there a law that protects franchisees in India?

India has no franchise-specific law and no mandatory disclosure document. Your rights come mainly from the franchise agreement, backed by general laws such as the Indian Contract Act. Misrepresentation can still give you a claim, but disputes are slow and costly. That is why reading the contract with a lawyer before paying matters more than any promise in a sales meeting.

Franchise or own brand: which is better for a first-time owner?

A franchise saves time on menu building and can bring ready demand if the brand is already strong near you. An own brand keeps all margin and control but needs more work to gain traction. If no proven franchise sells well in your delivery radius, an own brand with a tight, well-priced delivery menu is often the safer first step.
