Across 12 PFC client case studies, restaurants starting from a small online base grew monthly Swiggy and Zomato sales by a median of 6.5x (range 3.2x to 29x). Established kitchens grew by a median of about 1.7x. In all seven cases with order counts, more orders, not bigger bills, drove most of the growth.

This is first-party data from our own client reports. Where we computed something (a median, a ratio), the inputs are shown. These are individual client outcomes, not guarantees.

Method: what we excluded and why

  • Not a random sample. These are clients with documented data who agreed to be named, so the set leans towards cases that went well.
  • Excluded outlets. One outlet whose sales fell below its starting month, and one where the same Zomato screenshot appears on two clients' slides (we also dropped Regenta Inn's Zomato figures for that reason).
  • Excluded reports. A second Drama deck whose figures do not reconcile with the register report, and an older Ivoryy monthly claim that the client deck does not support.
  • Excluded outliers. Three Swiggy ad ROI readings flagged as outliers in the client reports: 84.3x (Burrito Bros), and 92.6x and 55.7x (Mohan Sweets). Burrito Bros' second brand is also left out of its headline.
  • Partial periods. Part weeks and part months are left out of growth comparisons or compared as daily run-rates. The same rule applies to ad ROI: part-month readings are left out of the ROI ranges below.
  • Rounded figures. Mohan Sweets' fifth-month figures (₹4.48 lakh Zomato, ₹2.23 lakh Swiggy, 1,623 and 825 orders) come from report text and are rounded. Its 29x and 12.9x multiples and its ₹274 AOV rest on them, so treat those as approximate.
  • Combined figures. For Pasta Express we added both platforms: ₹47,264 + ₹18,244 = ₹65,508 rising to ₹2,21,291 + ₹48,941 = ₹2,70,232, or 4.1x. For Ivoryy, ₹4,20,229 over 11 days (about ₹38,200 a day) became ₹21,54,295 over 19 days (about ₹1,13,400 a day), or 3.0x.

Ad ROI ranges on Swiggy vs Zomato

All ROI figures here are as reported by the platforms' ad dashboards. They are not reconciled to net sales, and the spread across months is uneven, so we give ranges, not averages. Flagged outliers and part-month readings are excluded; only full PFC months are counted.

  • Swiggy, monthly ROI across full PFC months: 2.9x to 16.6x. Punjabi Rasoi 4.6x to 14.4x, Burrito Bros 3.4x to 13.9x, Mohan Sweets 2.9x to 12.1x, Olantic 8 4.6x to 8.9x, Balaji Dhaba 15.8x to 16.6x (ads GMV ÷ ad spend).
  • Zomato, monthly ROI across full PFC months: 1.7x to 9.3x. Punjabi Rasoi 5.6x to 8.0x, Olantic 8 2.8x to 9.3x, Burrito Bros 2.4x to 6.1x, Mohan Sweets 1.7x to 6.0x.
  • Head to head: in the four outlets reporting both platforms, Swiggy's reported ROI was higher in 22 of 29 matching months (Punjabi Rasoi 9 of 14, Burrito Bros 5 of 5, Mohan Sweets 3 of 3, Olantic 8 5 of 7).
  • Early readings drift down as budgets grow. Olantic 8's Zomato ROI went from 9.3x on ₹398 of spend to 4.8x on ₹36.7K.

Drama's register report gives a separate, incremental measure: each extra ₹1 of ad spend went with ₹5.67 more net sale on Swiggy and ₹2.29 on Zomato. Same direction, but not comparable with dashboard ROI.

Ops signals in the stronger cases

The outlets that scaled smoothly kept the kitchen online, fast and reliable as volume rose:

  • Availability around 99%. Balaji Dhaba 99% on Swiggy and 98.3% to 99.9% a month on Zomato; Olantic 8 99%+ on both platforms from its fourth month; Mohan Sweets lifted Zomato availability from 77.9% in the month before PFC to 99%+ by its second month.
  • Single-digit or near single-digit prep time. Mohan Sweets 2 to 4 minutes on Zomato, Balaji Dhaba 8 to 10 minutes (10-minute average on Swiggy), Burrito Bros down from 14 to 8 minutes, Olantic 8 down from 26 to 11 minutes.
  • Lost sales near zero. Burrito Bros had zero lost sales and zero cancellations on both apps from its fourth month. Olantic 8's Swiggy lost sales went from ₹1,564 to ₹0. Punjabi Rasoi's Zomato lost sales fell from ₹27.9K to ₹320 within two months. Balaji Dhaba rejected 8 of 7,363 Swiggy orders (0.1%).

Ratings are a separate story. Only Burrito Bros had rating repair as an explicit PFC action (Zomato 1.8 to 4.3, through rating drives and complaint handling). We do not credit ratings elsewhere.

Discount depth vs growth

Deeper discounts can buy volume, but several cases grew while discounts got shallower.

  • Punjabi Rasoi: Swiggy effective discount fell from 27.3% to 13.4% over nine months while Swiggy monthly sales rose from ₹7,83,375 to ₹13,53,000 (+73%). Zomato discount went from 15–18% to 11–13% over a shorter stretch.
  • Balaji Dhaba (Swiggy): discount spend held near 24% of revenue for two full months, then came in at 22.1% in the first ten days of the latest month while daily revenue kept rising.
  • Olantic 8: used 70:30 discount sharing with Swiggy (Swiggy funds 30%), ₹99 items and freebies on a minimum order instead of blanket discounts.
  • Mohan Sweets: the report does not give discount depth for the growth months. When the owners later asked for margin over volume, deep codes were removed and moved to flat, approved discounts.
  • Limitless: discount rose from ₹2.28 lakh to ₹14.44 lakh from one month to the next. That is why we quote its net order value (₹5.23 lakh to ₹18.00 lakh) rather than gross sales value, and why it carries a caveat.

How to use these benchmarks

  1. Find your starting point. If you are at about ₹1.1 lakh a month or less online, or launching fresh, six of our eight small-base cases landed between 3.2x and 8.4x. If you are already established, our six data points ran from 1.13x to 3.4x, with a median of about 1.7x.
  2. Check your ops first. Compare your availability, prep time and lost sales with the stronger cases above (about 99% online, 2 to 11 minutes of prep, lost sales near zero). Close those gaps before raising ad budgets.
  3. Track orders and AOV separately. If sales are up but orders are flat, you are leaning on price or basket, which is harder to sustain.
  4. Read ROI with care. Use dashboard ROI to compare campaigns, not as profit. Expect early high readings to settle as spend grows.
  5. Watch discount depth every month. Growth that needs ever-deeper discounts is the thing to question.

Frequently asked questions

How much can a restaurant grow on Swiggy and Zomato in six months?

In our dataset, small-base outlets grew by a median of 6.5x and established outlets by a median of about 1.7x, over windows of two to six months or more. Windows differ, so the multiples are not directly comparable. These are individual client outcomes, not guarantees.

Is Swiggy or Zomato better for ad returns?

In four outlets running ads on both, Swiggy's dashboard ROI was higher in 22 of 29 matching months. Both figures are platform-reported and not reconciled to net sales, so test on your own outlet.

Should I raise prices to grow online sales?

Our cases point to volume. Orders rose far faster than AOV wherever we had counts, and Burrito Bros grew sales 7.6x while AOV fell 29%.

Do I need heavy discounts to grow?

Not necessarily. Punjabi Rasoi grew Swiggy sales 73% while cutting effective discount from 27.3% to 13.4%. Discount-led growth, as at Limitless, needs closer checks on margin.

Why are these not average industry figures?

They are PFC client cases, not a random sample, and the comparison types differ. Use them as reference points, not market averages.
