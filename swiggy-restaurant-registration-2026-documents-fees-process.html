Who can register a restaurant on Swiggy?

Any food business that can legally prepare and sell food from a fixed address can apply. That includes dine-in restaurants, cafes, QSRs, bakeries, sweet shops, cloud kitchens and home-based food businesses that hold valid FSSAI papers. Swiggy has to be able to deliver to your area, so serviceability of your address is checked during sign-up.

The basic eligibility test is simple. You need a kitchen address that matches your documents, a person or entity that owns the business, and a bank account in that name for payouts.

Is FSSAI registration enough, or do you need a licence?

Swiggy needs a valid FSSAI certificate. Which type you hold depends on your annual turnover, and those limits changed this year. Under the Food Safety and Standards (Licensing and Registration of Food Businesses) Amendment Regulations, 2026, the thresholds were raised with effect from 1 April 2026, as summarised in this legal update on the revised FSSAI limits.

FSSAI categoryTurnover limit before 1 April 2026Turnover limit from 1 April 2026
Basic registrationUp to ₹12 lakhUp to ₹1.5 crore
State licence₹12 lakh to ₹20 crore₹1.5 crore to ₹50 crore
Central licenceAbove ₹20 croreAbove ₹50 crore

So a new cloud kitchen with modest projected sales can usually list with a basic FSSAI registration. What matters is that the category fits your turnover and that the address on the certificate is the kitchen you are cooking from. Confirm your category on the FoSCoS portal or with your consultant before applying.

Can you register on Swiggy without GST?

This is the most searched question, and the honest answer has two parts.

On tax, the rules changed on 1 January 2022. Since then, food delivery platforms pay 5% GST on restaurant services ordered through them, instead of the restaurant, as Inc42 reported when the GST Council approved the change. That is why some small kitchens below the GST threshold ask whether they need a GSTIN at all.

On onboarding, Swiggy's published guide still lists a GST certificate among the documents. Whether the sign-up flow currently accepts a non-GST outlet, and what declaration it asks for, is shown during sign-up and can change. Do not rely on a blog, including this one, for your tax position. Ask your CA whether you should register for GST, and check what the partner portal asks for on the day you apply. If you plan to grow past a single small kitchen, getting a GSTIN early usually saves rework later.

How to register on Swiggy, step by step

  1. Start the application. Go to partner.swiggy.com or download the Swiggy Partner app and choose to register a new outlet. Use the mobile number and email you want as your permanent login.
  2. Enter outlet details. Restaurant name, exact address with map pin, cuisine, outlet type (dine-in, delivery-only, cloud kitchen) and operating hours.
  3. Upload documents. FSSAI, PAN, GST (if applicable), bank details and KYC. Upload clean scans, not phone photos with shadows.
  4. Add the menu. Items, prices, categories, add-ons and photos. Some cities do this through the portal, others through a Swiggy onboarding team.
  5. Verification. Swiggy checks documents and may ask for a takeout bill or visit the outlet. Its guide describes an employee visit to verify details and sign the agreement.
  6. Accept the agreement. Read the commission, fees and payout terms before you sign. Save a copy.
  7. Set up the order device. Log in to the Partner app on the phone or tablet that will sit in the kitchen. Test notifications and order acceptance.
  8. Go live. Once activated, your outlet becomes visible to customers in your delivery radius.

How long does Swiggy take to go live?

Swiggy does not publish a fixed timeline. The form itself takes minutes. Verification, menu set-up and activation depend on how clean your documents are and how busy the local onboarding team is. In our experience, applications with matching names and addresses move in days. Applications with a mismatch can sit for weeks while you go back and forth.

What commission will you pay on Swiggy?

Commission is not one fixed number. It depends on your contract, city, outlet type and any services you opt into. Reports put restaurant commission on Swiggy generally at 17–25%, according to a November 2025 Verdict Foodservice report citing CNBC-TV18. The same report notes a 2% collection fee introduced in February 2025 and a per-order fee on Swiggy One orders for select partners from 25 November 2025.

Why Swiggy applications get stuck or rejected

  • Address mismatch. FSSAI says one address, GST another, and the map pin a third.
  • Name mismatch. PAN is in the owner's name but the bank account is in a firm's name, or the other way round.
  • Expired or wrong-category FSSAI. A registration that has lapsed, or a category that does not fit your declared turnover.
  • Unserviceable location. The address falls outside a zone Swiggy currently delivers from.
  • Duplicate listing. The same address is already linked to another outlet or an old account.
  • Poor menu data. Missing prices, unclear item names or unusable photos hold up menu approval.

New outlets often get early exposure while the platform tests how customers respond to them.
