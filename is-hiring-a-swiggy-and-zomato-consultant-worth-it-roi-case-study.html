Why Restaurants Consider Hiring a Consultant

Swiggy and Zomato are competitive marketplaces.

Many restaurants experience:

  • Low visibility
  • High commission pressure (base commission is commonly in the range of 18–27% on Swiggy and 18–28% on Zomato, and varies by contract)
  • Poor ad ROI
  • Stagnant order volume

Without structured optimisation, performance often plateaus.

Both are valuable, but they serve different business needs.

When Hiring a Consultant Makes Sense

Ideal Situations

  • Orders are stagnant or declining
  • Heavy reliance on discounts
  • High ad spend with low ROI
  • Expansion plans

When It May Not Be Necessary

  • Early experimentation stage
  • Very small scale operations
  • Strong in-house expertise

Decision Framework

Ask these questions:

1. Are your orders growing consistently?

If not, structured optimisation can help.

2. Do you understand your profit per order?

Many restaurants track revenue but not margin.

3. Are ads generating positive ROI?

Ad spend without analysis often wastes budget.

4. Do you know which menu items generate profit?

Menu mix strongly affects margins.

If multiple answers are unclear, consulting can provide clarity. Book a growth audit.

Common Misconceptions About Consultants

Myth 1: Consultants only give advice

Strong consultants implement structured changes.

Myth 2: Discounts are the only growth lever

Menu engineering often provides better long-term results.

Myth 3: Ads alone solve visibility issues

Listing quality and ratings also matter.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a Swiggy and Zomato consultant cost?

What results can a restaurant expect from aggregator consulting?

Restaurants that implement structured aggregator optimisation often see order increases of 30–120% and AOV increases of 20–50%, along with lower dependence on discounts. Illustrative; results vary and are not guaranteed.

What does an aggregator consultant work on?

Listing optimisation, menu engineering, pricing strategy, ad optimisation and guidance on what the restaurant can do for its ratings, all aimed at visibility, conversion and profit per order.

When is hiring a consultant not necessary?

If you are still in an early experimentation stage, operate at a very small scale, or already have strong in-house aggregator expertise, a consultant may not be needed.

How do I know if my restaurant needs help?

Check whether orders are growing consistently, whether you know your profit per order, whether ads show positive ROI and which menu items make money. If several of these are unclear, an external review can help.
