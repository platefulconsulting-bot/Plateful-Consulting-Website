Price your delivery menu so every order still makes money after platform commission, GST on that commission, packaging and discount share. Delivery menu pricing should account for platform commissions, food cost, packaging cost and customer price perception. The objective is to:

  • maintain profitability after commission
  • remain competitive within category pricing bands
  • increase average order value (AOV)
  • reduce reliance on heavy discounting

Why Delivery Pricing Requires a Different Strategy

Delivery platforms introduce cost layers that do not exist in dine-in operations. Key cost components include:

  • platform commission: Swiggy typically 18–27% and Zomato 18–28% base commission in contracts PFC audits; press reports put the range at roughly 16–30%
  • GST on commission (charged at 18%)
  • packaging cost
  • ad spend allocation
  • discount participation

If pricing does not account for these costs, restaurants often see margin erosion despite high order volume. The legitimate levers here are your own menu prices, portion and combo design, packaging choices and how much discount you agree to fund, plus whatever terms you can negotiate openly in your platform contract.

Dine-In vs Delivery Pricing

Many restaurant owners hesitate to price delivery items higher than dine-in. However, delivery customers generally accept slightly higher pricing in exchange for convenience, and the practice is widespread: a Jefferies survey of 80 restaurants across eight cities, reported in 2022, found 80% priced at least some items higher on delivery apps than on their dine-in menus, with a median markup of about 10 to 11%.

Commission-Adjusted Pricing Formula

Pricing decisions should be based on contribution margin, not on the dine-in menu.

Basic Pricing Formula

Delivery price = (food cost + packaging cost + overhead allocation) ÷ (1 – commission %)

GST at 18% is also charged on the platform's commission, so it is worth checking how that affects your effective commission cost when you run the formula.

Required price ≈ ₹320–₹340. Your own overheads, GST on commission and discount share will change the answer.

Why This Matters

Without commission-adjusted pricing, restaurants may unknowingly operate at low margins. Even small price adjustments can make a meaningful difference to profitability.

Customers often compare prices across restaurants before ordering. Key comparison factors:

  • combo value
  • discount availability

Pricing well above the category average reduces conversion. Pricing well below average reduces perceived quality. The best price balances value and perception.

Frequently asked questions

Should delivery prices be higher than dine-in?

Usually yes. In our audits most restaurants price delivery menus 10 to 20% higher to cover platform commission, GST on that commission and packaging cost.

How much markup is acceptable for delivery?

Typically 10 to 20%, depending on cuisine category and local competition. High-demand items may need a smaller markup to stay competitive.

Should all items be marked up equally?

Not necessarily. Work out each item with the commission-adjusted formula, then check it against the category price band before deciding.

Do customers compare prices before ordering?

Yes. Customers often compare listings on price per portion, combo value and discount availability, so pricing far above or below the category average hurts.

How often should pricing be reviewed?

Every 30 to 60 days, and whenever food costs, packaging or your commission terms change.
