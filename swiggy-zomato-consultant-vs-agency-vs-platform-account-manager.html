A restaurant consultant, a digital marketing agency and your Swiggy or Zomato account manager can all help you grow delivery sales, but each serves a different interest. The account manager is free and works for the platform. An agency mostly runs social media. A specialist consultant works on your listing, menu, ads and kitchen data for a fee.

Doing it yourself is the fourth option, and for many small outlets it is the right one at the start. This guide compares all four fairly: who pays them, what they actually touch, how they charge, the red flags to watch for and which fits your stage.

Whose interest does each one serve?

The platform account manager

Your account manager is a platform employee. That is not a bad thing. They can explain new features, push a stuck ticket and tell you which programmes your outlet qualifies for. A Swiggy blog post profiling one of its account managers describes advice on discounting, ads, dish photos, pricing and add-ons. That is genuinely useful guidance.

But be clear about the incentive. Swiggy's Q1 FY2027 shareholder letter lists its food delivery revenue as pre-agreed commissions plus advertising revenue from restaurant partners, plus user fees. So when an account manager suggests more ad spend or a deeper offer, the advice may also help you, but it is not neutral. Listen, then check the numbers yourself.

The digital marketing agency

A general agency works for you, but within what it sells. Most are strong on Instagram content, reels, Google Business Profile and websites. Few work daily inside the Swiggy and Zomato partner dashboards, where most delivery orders are actually decided.

The specialist restaurant consultant

A specialist consultant is paid by you, so in principle works for you. In practice, the fee model shapes behaviour. A consultant paid on a share of sales may chase top-line growth even when heavy discounts hurt your margin. A flat retainer can drift into monthly reports with little action. Neither is wrong by default. You just need to know which pressure applies. Our explainer on what a restaurant consultant actually does covers the role in more detail.

Doing it yourself

DIY serves only you. The cost is time and blind spots. Many owners never compare their numbers against peers in the same locality, so they cannot tell whether 20 orders a day is good or poor for their cuisine and area.

How do restaurant consultants charge in India?

People often search "how much do food consultants charge". The honest answer is that there is no reliable public rate card for Swiggy and Zomato growth work. Most fee numbers online come from vendors advertising their own services, and many relate to opening a restaurant or designing a kitchen, not aggregator growth. So we will describe the common models rather than quote figures.

ModelHow it worksSuitsWatch out for
One-time audit or fixed projectA set fee for a review of your listing, menu, ads and ops, with a written action planOwners who want to execute themselvesPlans that are generic and not built from your own dashboard data
Monthly retainerA fixed monthly fee for ongoing management and reviewsOutlets that need weekly hands-on workReports without changes; no clear exit terms
Fixed-term plan with handoverA set period, often a few months, ending with your team trained to run thingsOwners who want to build in-house skillHandover that is only a slide deck
Performance-linkedA share of incremental sales above an agreed baseline, sometimes with a smaller fixed feeOutlets with stable history to measure againstVague baselines, seasonality, and growth bought through margin-killing discounts

On performance-linked deals, write down the baseline, the period, whether ad and discount spend is deducted and any cap. Outlook Business reported in June 2025 that some restaurants said a platform ad product priced as a share of incremental revenue led to much larger deductions than they expected. Whoever you hire, define "incremental" in writing before you start.

Red flags when hiring a restaurant consultant or agency

  • Guaranteed order numbers. Nobody controls the platform algorithm, customer demand or your kitchen on a busy Friday. Promises of fixed results are a sales line.
  • Secret commission "deals". Anyone offering a hidden or under-the-table commission rate is a risk. Commission is set by your contract with the platform. Lower costs should come from legitimate levers: contract terms you negotiate openly, smarter offers and fewer cancellations.
  • No dashboard access, or no visibility for you. A serious adviser works from your actual partner dashboard data and shows you the same numbers. Be wary if someone will not share what they changed, or if you lose sight of your own account.
  • Vanity metrics. Impressions, followers and menu views are inputs. Ask for orders, sales, average order value, ad return and net payout.
  • More spend as the only answer. If every fix is a bigger ad budget or a deeper discount, ask what happens to your margin.
  • Long lock-ins with no exit clause. Reasonable engagements let either side leave with notice.

The same Outlook Business report described restaurants alleging ads and discounts ran on their listings without consent. Whoever manages your account, check your weekly payout statement line by line. Our guide on how to read your Swiggy and Zomato payout statement shows what to look for.

Questions to ask before you hire anyone

  1. Which restaurants like mine (cuisine, price, city) have you worked with, and can I speak to one?
  2. Will you work from my live Swiggy and Zomato dashboards, and will I see every change you make?
  3. What exactly do you do each week, and who on your team does it?
  4. Which metrics will you report, and how often? Do they include net payout, not just sales?
  5. How do you decide on ad spend and discounts, and how do you check if they paid off?
  6. What is the fee model, what is included, and what costs extra?
  7. If any part is performance-linked, what is the baseline and how is incremental growth measured?
  8. What is the notice period, and what do I keep if we stop (data, plans, creatives)?
  9. What will you need from my kitchen team, and how much time?

Frequently asked questions

Is the Swiggy or Zomato account manager enough on their own?

For many small outlets, yes, at least at first. The account manager is free and can explain features, programmes and escalations. The limits are that they cover one platform, work to the platform's targets and may not look closely at your margins or kitchen. If growth stalls even after following their advice, an independent review of your data is worth considering.

How much does a restaurant consultant cost in India?

There is no reliable public rate card for Swiggy and Zomato growth consulting. Fees depend on scope, number of outlets and the model: one-time audit, monthly retainer, fixed-term plan or performance-linked. Ask for a written proposal listing weekly tasks, reports, the notice period and what is excluded. Compare proposals on scope and accountability, not just the headline fee.

Is a performance-linked fee better than a fixed retainer?

Not automatically. A performance-linked fee aligns incentives only if the baseline, period, seasonality and treatment of ad and discount spend are written down. Without that, you may pay for growth that would have happened anyway, or for sales bought through deep discounts. A fixed fee is simpler to budget. Choose based on how clearly results can be measured for your outlet.

Can a digital marketing agency handle Swiggy and Zomato growth?

Some can, but many general agencies focus on Instagram, Google and websites. Delivery growth depends heavily on work inside the partner dashboards: listing quality, menu structure, offers, ad bidding, availability and prep time. Ask any agency to show you how they would read your dashboard and which delivery metrics they have improved for similar restaurants before you hand them this work.

Can a consultant reduce the commission I pay?

Commission is set in your contract with each platform and varies by city, restaurant and terms. A good adviser can help you understand that contract and reduce your total cost through legitimate levers, such as better offer choices, fewer cancellations and smarter ad spend. Be wary of anyone promising a secret or under-the-table commission rate. That is a red flag, not a service.
