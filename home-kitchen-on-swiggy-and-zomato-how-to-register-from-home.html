Yes, you can apply. Zomato registration for home kitchen owners uses the same partner flow as restaurants, and Swiggy registration for home kitchen sellers works the same way. You need FSSAI registration for your home address, PAN, a bank account and a menu with photos. GSTIN depends on turnover and platform. Neither app publishes a separate home-kitchen sign-up route.

Do Swiggy and Zomato accept home kitchens in 2026?

Neither platform publishes a rule that bars a home kitchen. Neither offers a dedicated home-kitchen onboarding route either. You apply as a food business, like a restaurant or cloud kitchen, and the platform checks your documents, address and menu.

Both companies have tried home-food models before, with mixed results:

  • Zomato and Tinmen. Zomato invested in Tinmen, a Hyderabad startup that delivered lunches cooked by home chefs, in 2017. Inc42 reported that Tinmen shut from 1 December 2019.
  • Swiggy Daily. It launched in June 2019 with meals from home chefs, tiffin services and organised vendors, as Republic World reported. Inc42 reported it closed during the pandemic and restarted in May 2024 as a homestyle meal service. We could not find a 2025 or 2026 source confirming whether Daily still runs today.
  • Zomato Everyday. This homestyle meal feature was shut in May 2025. The company said demand was a niche use case, mostly around offices in metros, according to Outlook Business.

So in practice, a home kitchen goes through the normal onboarding. From what we see, approval comes down to four things: a valid FSSAI registration at the address you list, an address the delivery fleet can serve, a clean menu with real photos, and the local city team's checks. Some applicants get a verification call or visit. Plan for it.

FSSAI registration for home kitchen: what changed in 2026

FSSAI registration is the one document you cannot skip. Swiggy's partner guide lists an FSSAI certificate among the documents it needs, and Zomato's online-ordering terms ask partners for FSSAI, PAN and GSTIN details.

The rules got simpler this year. According to the FSSAI press release of 13 March 2026, from 1 April 2026:

  • The turnover limit for basic registration went up from ₹12 lakh to ₹1.5 crore. State licences now cover businesses up to ₹50 crore.
  • Registrations and licences now have perpetual validity, so you no longer renew them every year or few years.
  • For small operators, FSSAI says there is no pre-inspection, registration is instant and fees are lower.

Almost every home food business will fall under basic registration on the FoSCoS portal. Usually you need a photo ID, a passport-size photo, proof that you occupy the premises and a list of the foods you will make. For a rented flat, that proof is the rent agreement. For an owned home, a sale deed or electricity bill works. The address on FoSCoS should match your proof word for word. Check the current fee on FoSCoS before you pay, and avoid agents who charge heavily for a form you can file yourself.

Do you need GST for a home food business?

Under tax law, often not at the start, but it depends on your turnover. Since 1 January 2022, the platform pays GST on restaurant services sold through it. That includes food from unregistered sellers, as set out in CBIC Circular 167/23/2021-GST. The same circular says your platform sales still count in your aggregate turnover for the registration threshold.

For services, that threshold is generally ₹20 lakh a year, and lower in a few special-category states. Platform document lists are a separate question. Swiggy's partner FAQ has said partners below ₹20 lakh can declare that they have no GSTIN, while Zomato's online-ordering terms list GSTIN among required details. Check what each partner app asks for when you sign up. Remember that WhatsApp orders, tiffin subscriptions and catering also add to turnover. Get a CA to confirm your position. Local permissions: society, landlord and city rules

Platforms rarely ask for these, but they decide whether you can keep running. Many housing societies and rent agreements restrict commercial use of a flat. A steady stream of delivery riders at the gate will get noticed. Talk to the society committee before you go live, and get a written no-objection if you can.

Municipal trade or health licences for home food units vary a lot between cities. Some corporations expect one, others do not enforce it for small home units. Ask your ward office. Domestic LPG is meant for household use, so ask your gas distributor about a commercial connection once you cook at volume.

Kitchen hygiene and packaging basics

  • Keep a separate shelf, fridge section and utensils for business food.
  • Store raw and cooked food apart, and label prepared items with the date.
  • Wear a cap and apron, and keep pets out of the cooking area.
  • Use tamper-proof, leak-proof containers sized for each dish. Gravies need sealed tubs, not foil.
  • Print your FSSAI number on a sticker or label for every pack.
  • Add a small thank-you note. Home kitchens win on the personal touch.

Leaks and soggy food are a common cause of bad reviews. Our guide on restaurant packaging for delivery covers container choices dish by dish.

Menu and pricing for a small home kitchen

A home cloud kitchen works best with a short menu. Eight to fifteen items is plenty. Lead with two or three hero dishes, add a combo or thali, and keep one dessert or drink to lift order value. Every extra item adds stock, waste and prep time.

Price from the payout backwards. Platform commission depends on your contract, city and services. On top of it come GST handled by the platform, packaging, payment and ad costs, and any discount you fund. Run your prices through our commission calculator before you publish the menu. If a dish loses money once an offer runs, fix the price or keep it out of offers.

When should you move to a commercial cloud kitchen?

Move when the home setup becomes the limit, not the demand. The common signs:

  • You are turning orders off at lunch or dinner because the stove or fridge cannot keep up.
  • Prep times are slipping and late-delivery complaints are rising.
  • The society or landlord is raising objections.
  • Your turnover is close to the GST threshold and you want proper books anyway.
  • You want to add a second brand or a second cuisine.

Move only after the menu has steady repeat customers and a stable rating. Shifting a weak menu to an expensive kitchen just raises your losses.
