Punjabi Rasoi, a high-volume North Indian kitchen in Rohini, Delhi, climbed from a winter low of ₹20.1 lakh to ₹34.3 lakh a month on Swiggy and Zomato (+71%). Swiggy discount depth fell from 27.3% to 13.4%, Swiggy ad ROI peaked at 14.4x, and a new Noida outlet grew 5.4x in three months.

The client and the challenge

Punjabi Rasoi is not a small restaurant that needed to be discovered. When our monthly reporting starts, the Rohini outlet was already doing close to ₹27 lakh a month online across Swiggy and Zomato, with more than 4,500 orders. At that size the problem changes. Getting more orders is possible. Getting more orders without handing back the margin through deep discounts and wasteful ad spend is the real work.

The brand also had a familiar seasonal pattern. Sales dropped through the winter months, bottoming out at ₹20.1 lakh in the weakest month, with average order value sliding to ₹536. The brief was to pull the kitchen out of that dip, keep discounts under control while doing it, and launch a second outlet in Noida.

A note on honesty before the numbers. PFC was already working with the brand before the first report in this data set, so there is no pre-PFC baseline here. Two monthly reports are also missing from the record. And this is not a straight-line growth story: an earlier month at ₹39.5 lakh is still the best month on record, and the latest month is about 13% below it. What the data does show clearly is a strong recovery, cheaper growth and a successful second launch.

What PFC did

1. Ads run on return, not on habit

Ad budgets were judged on what they brought back, platform by platform. Zomato ad ROI stayed between 6x and 8x in every reported month from the winter onward. On Swiggy, ROI reached 11.9x and then 14.4x in back-to-back summer months, while ad spend came down from ₹70,000 to ₹52,000. All ROI figures here are as reported by the platforms' ad dashboards.

Swiggy ROI was not smooth. It dipped to 4.6x in one spring month before recovering. That is normal at this volume, and it is why ads need a monthly review rather than a set-and-forget budget.

2. Discount depth cut while sales grew

This is the part most high-volume kitchens get wrong. When sales dip, the reflex is to deepen the offer. Punjabi Rasoi did the opposite. Swiggy effective discount went from 27.3% at the peak of winter to 13.4% in the latest month. On Zomato, effective discount came down from 15–18% in the spring to 11–13% by mid-summer. Over the same stretch, monthly sales rose by 71%.

3. Lost sales tracked every month

Every monthly review covered lost sales, item availability and complaints. The clearest example: Zomato lost sales were ₹27,900 in the first winter month and down to ₹320 two months later. At this order volume, items going out of stock or the kitchen going offline at peak hours quietly costs more than most owners expect.

4. A second outlet launched on both apps

PFC launched and scaled Punjabi Rasoi Noida on both Swiggy and Zomato. The monthly numbers are in the Noida section below.

What other high-volume restaurants can take from this

  • At scale, the win is profitable growth. A kitchen doing ₹20 lakh+ a month does not need more orders at any cost. It needs each extra rupee of sales to keep more of its margin.
  • Cutting discounts does not have to cut sales. Punjabi Rasoi halved its Swiggy effective discount over the same months its sales recovered by 71%.
  • Judge ads on return every month. Swiggy ROI moved between 4.6x and 14.4x in the same year. A monthly review is how you catch the weak months early.
  • Lost sales are a hidden leak. Tracking availability and outages brought Zomato lost sales from ₹27,900 to ₹320 in two months.
  • A second outlet can scale quickly on both apps. Punjabi Rasoi Noida grew 5.4x in its first three months.
  • Be honest about seasonality. An earlier month is still the best on record. Growth plans should account for winter dips rather than pretend they will not happen.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Punjabi Rasoi Rohini sell on Swiggy and Zomato?

In the latest reported month, the Rohini outlet did ₹34.3 lakh in online sales: ₹20.74 lakh on Zomato and ₹13.53 lakh on Swiggy, across 5,398 orders (a count that includes a derived Swiggy order figure). Across 16 reported months, its total online sales were ₹4.26 crore.

Did Punjabi Rasoi grow by giving bigger discounts?

No. Swiggy effective discount fell from 27.3% to 13.4% and Zomato effective discount fell from 15–18% to 11–13%, while monthly sales rose 71% from the winter low. Average order value also went up, from ₹536 to ₹635.

What ad returns did Punjabi Rasoi get on Swiggy and Zomato?

As reported by the platform ad dashboards, Zomato ad ROI stayed between 6x and 8x from the winter onward, and Swiggy ad ROI reached 11.9x and then 14.4x in two consecutive summer months while ad spend came down from ₹70,000 to ₹52,000.

How fast did the new Noida outlet grow?

Punjabi Rasoi Noida went from ₹1.11 lakh in its launch month to ₹6.03 lakh in its fourth month, a 5.4x increase in three months. Orders grew from 140 to 854 a month.

Is this the best month Punjabi Rasoi has ever had?

No. An earlier month at ₹39.5 lakh is still the best on record, and the latest month is about 13% below it. The story here is the recovery from a winter low, shallower discounts and a successful second outlet, not year-on-year growth. There is also no pre-PFC baseline in the data.
