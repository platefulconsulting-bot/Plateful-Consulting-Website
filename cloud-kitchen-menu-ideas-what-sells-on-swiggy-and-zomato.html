How many items should a cloud kitchen menu launch with?

Neither Swiggy nor Zomato fixes a number. In our experience, 20 to 30 items across four to six categories is enough for a single-brand launch. Many new kitchens list 70 to 100 items and pay for it in wastage, slower prep and confused customers.

A tight menu helps in three ways:

  • Faster prep. Fewer items means fewer base preparations and quicker handover to riders.
  • Lower wastage. Shared ingredients across dishes keep inventory small and fresh.
  • Easier choice. Customers scroll on a phone. A short, clear menu converts better than a long one.

Use 10 to 15 core ingredients to build most dishes. Then cut any item that sells poorly for six to eight weeks. Our guide to menu optimisation for delivery covers category order, names and descriptions in detail.

Hero items and combos: how to build cloud kitchen menu ideas that sell

Every brand needs three or four hero dishes. These are the items you photograph best, place at the top and name clearly. They should be things you can make the same way every single time.

Combos do the rest. A biryani brand might sell biryani, raita, salan and a dessert as one meal. A rolls brand might pair two rolls with a drink. Combos raise order value, make the choice easier and let you control margins better than single items.

Good combo rules:

  • Build combos around your hero item, not slow sellers.
  • Offer one solo meal and one meal for two.
  • Keep add-ons small and cheap to make, such as raita, dips, drinks or a sweet.
  • Name the combo for the occasion, such as "office lunch box" or "late-night meal".

How should you price a cloud kitchen menu for delivery?

Delivery pricing has more deductions than dine-in pricing. Before you set a price, add up food cost, packaging, platform commission, any discount you plan to run and payment charges. Commission is a percentage set in your contract and varies by city, partner type and terms, so do not copy a number from someone else. Put your own figures into our commission calculator to see what you actually keep per order.

A simple order of work:

  1. Cost every recipe down to the gram, including oil and garnish.
  2. Add packaging cost per order, not per item.
  3. Run the price through the commission and discount maths.
  4. Check the result against two or three competitors in your delivery radius.
  5. Adjust portion size or the combo before you cut the price.

For the detail on price points, anchors and discount planning, read our menu pricing strategy for online delivery and the post on food cost control on delivery.

Does your menu fit your packaging?

Packaging is part of the menu, not an afterthought. Decide the container when you design the dish. Gravies need leak-proof, tamper-evident containers. Fried items need venting so they stay crisp. Burgers do better with sauce on the side. Keep hot and cold items in separate compartments or bags.

Test every dish with a real 30-minute ride before launch. Open it, photograph it and compare it with your menu photo. If it looks different, fix the dish or the box. Our post on packaging for delivery lists the common failures we see in reviews.

Can one kitchen run multiple brands?

Yes. Many cloud kitchens run two to four delivery brands from one kitchen. Each brand gets its own listing, name and menu on Swiggy and Zomato, which gives you more shelf space in search results. Confirm the listing and licensing requirements for each brand during onboarding, since these depend on the platform and your setup.

Pick brands that share ingredients and equipment. A North Indian curry brand and a biryani brand can share gravies, onions and proteins. A rolls brand can use the same tikka. A dessert brand adds orders late at night with little extra prep.

Do not launch all brands at once. Get the first one to steady ratings and prep times, then add the next. A second brand that slows the kitchen will hurt the first one's ratings too.

Cloud kitchen menu launch checklist

  1. Pick one concept based on demand data and your local competition.
  2. Choose three or four hero items you can make consistently.
  3. Limit the launch menu to 20 to 30 items in four to six categories.
  4. Build two or three combos around the hero items.
  5. Cost every recipe and run prices through the commission calculator.
  6. Match each dish to a tested container.
  7. Shoot real photos of the food as it arrives, not as it leaves the pan.
  8. Write short, clear descriptions with portion size and spice level.
  9. Review item-wise sales after six to eight weeks and cut the weak ones.
  10. Add a second brand only when prep times and ratings are stable.
