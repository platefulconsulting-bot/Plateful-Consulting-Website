A direct channel pays off when you have repeat customers who already know you, most orders fall in a tight delivery radius, and you can keep the switching discount at about 10% or less. On a ₹500 order, a direct order typically costs about ₹100–110 against roughly ₹159 on an aggregator once commission, GST and payment fees are counted.

So direct ordering is usually cheaper per order, but by less than most owners expect. Once you pay for delivery, payment processing, software and the discount needed to make customers switch, the saving is closer to 10% of the order value than the 25% many owners assume. Direct ordering works when three things are true:

  • You have a base of repeat customers who already know you.
  • Most orders fall within a tight delivery radius, so delivery stays cheap.
  • You can keep the switching discount at or below about 10%.

Without those, a direct channel costs time and money and moves few orders.

Commission disappears on a direct order, but other costs appear. The comparison below uses a ₹500 order and illustrative rates; replace them with your own quotes.

The maths: aggregator ₹125 + ₹22.50 + ₹10 + ₹1.80 = ₹159.30; direct ₹60 + ₹10 + ₹1.80 + ₹10 + ₹25 = ₹106.80. That is a saving of about ₹52 per order, or roughly 10.5% of the order value. It is real money, but not the 25% many owners assume when they think "no commission". Aggregator payment processing is not free: Swiggy charges a 2% collection fee and Zomato charges a payment mechanism fee, which is why it appears in both columns.

The saving disappears quickly if the switching discount grows. At 16% off (₹80), the direct order costs ₹161.80, more than the aggregator. The break-even sits at a discount of roughly 15.5%. A direct channel is not an excuse to discount; it is a way to keep customers who would come back anyway.

The Fixed-Cost Break-Even

A direct channel also carries monthly fixed costs: software subscription, a phone or WhatsApp line someone has to answer, and marketing to keep customers using it. Work out how many direct orders a month you need to cover them:

Direct orders needed per month = fixed monthly cost ÷ saving per order

With ₹10,000 a month in fixed costs and a ₹52.50 saving per order, you need about 190 direct orders a month (₹10,000 ÷ ₹52.50), or roughly 6–7 a day, before the channel earns anything. A restaurant doing 15 aggregator orders a day will struggle to reach that. One doing 80 has a realistic path. These figures are illustrative; results vary with your costs and volume.

Choosing Your Direct Channels

  • WhatsApp ordering. Lowest setup cost and the channel Indian customers already use. In our experience it works well up to a few dozen orders a day, then needs a catalogue or a bot to stay manageable.
  • Own website or ordering app. Gives you customer data and a branded experience. It only pays off with enough volume to cover the subscription, and customers have to be sent to it; it will not be found on its own.
  • Phone orders. Still common for bulk and repeat orders. Needs a disciplined process so orders are not lost at peak hours.
  • ONDC. A low-commission network that gives you discovery through third-party buyer apps. Demand is still thin: network retail orders fell about 30% between October 2024 and February 2025. See whether ONDC is worth listing on in 2026.
  • Rapido Ownly. A zero-commission delivery app for restaurants, live in Bengaluru and Hyderabad, with plans to reach all metros in October–December 2026 (ThePrint/PTI). It is a third-party app rather than your own channel, but it changes the cost comparison where it is live. See whether Rapido Ownly is worth it.

Moving Customers to Direct Without Breaking Rules

The cheapest direct customer is one who already eats your food. Build the channel from the places you own:

  • Dine-in guests. A table card or bill message with a WhatsApp number and a small first-order benefit.
  • Google Business Profile. Add your ordering link and phone number so people who search your name can order directly.
  • Instagram and your website. Link to the direct channel from your profile and posts.
  • Bulk and catering enquiries. Route these to direct booking by default.

Be careful with aggregator customers. Partner agreements restrict using platform orders to win customers away: Zomato's partner terms, for example, bar using customer data for unsolicited marketing or taking the same orders through other channels in parallel. Putting flyers for your direct channel inside aggregator packaging is the kind of thing that can fall foul of these rules. Read your agreement before you add anything to aggregator orders, because breaking it can put your listing at risk.

Watch your menu too. Zomato's terms require that items you make available through your own channels, such as your website or app, are also made available on Zomato. If you plan "direct-only" dishes, check your agreement first.

A 90-Day Rollout

  1. Days 1–30. Start lean. Launch WhatsApp ordering with a short menu and fixed delivery radius. Promote it only to dine-in guests and existing contacts.
  2. Days 31–60. Measure. Track direct orders per day, repeat rate, delivery cost per order and how many orders the discount actually brings in.
  3. Days 61–90. Decide. If you are on track to clear the fixed-cost break-even, invest in a website or ordering software. If not, keep WhatsApp as a convenience channel and focus effort on aggregator margin instead. See how to read your payout statement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is direct ordering cheaper than Swiggy and Zomato?

Per order, usually yes. In our illustrative ₹500 example, a direct order costs about ₹107 against about ₹159 on an aggregator, a saving of about ₹52. The saving shrinks fast if you need a large discount to make customers switch.

Can I leave Swiggy and Zomato and run only on direct orders?

Very few restaurants can. Aggregators bring in new customers; a direct channel mostly serves people who already know you. Most profitable restaurants use both and shift repeat orders toward direct over time.

How do I handle delivery for direct orders?

Use your own riders if order density is high and your radius is small, or a hyperlocal delivery partner charged per drop if volume is uneven. Get per-drop quotes for your actual radius before you price the channel.

What discount should I offer on direct orders?

Keep it small, around 5–10%, or use a non-cash benefit such as a free side. In our illustrative example, a discount above about 15% costs more than the commission and fees you are saving.

Can I sell dishes only on my own channel?

Check your agreement first. Zomato's partner terms require items available through your own channels to also be available on Zomato, so direct-only specials may breach your contract.
