If you want to know how to increase Zomato orders, work the funnel in order. Get seen more often in search, turn more menu opens into orders, and keep the kitchen online and reliable so the platform keeps showing you. Ads and offers help only after the listing converts. Fix leaks first, then pay for traffic.

Quick answer

  • Find the leak first. Check impressions, menu opens and orders separately. Each one has a different fix.
  • Stay online. Availability, prep time and rejections shape how often you are shown. Fix these before spending on ads.
  • Make the menu easy to buy from. Photos on best sellers, short clear categories, combos and honest pricing.
  • Use offers that protect margin. Minimum-order offers and combos usually beat blanket deep discounts.
  • Scale ads on returns. Zomato sponsored listings are charged per click, so a weak listing wastes budget.
  • Fix the causes of bad ratings. Cold food, spills, wrong items and delays. Replies alone do not move ratings.

The demand is there. In Eternal's Q2 FY27 results, Zomato's food delivery net order value grew 21.5% year on year to ₹11,445 crore, with about 28 million average monthly transacting customers, as reported by Inc42. The real question is why those customers pick the outlet next to you.

Where are you losing Zomato orders? The funnel table

Every Zomato order passes through three stages. A customer sees your listing, opens your menu, then places an order. Most owners look only at the final order count. That hides the real problem. Plenty of impressions with few menu opens is a different issue from lots of menu opens with few orders.

If menu opens are fine but orders are poor, buying more impressions with ads just buys more drop-offs. Fix the weakest stage first.

Lever 1: Visibility and ranking

Zomato does not publish its ranking formula. What we see across our audits is that the platform rewards listings that customers click, order from and rate well, and that stay reliably open. Visibility is earned through performance signals. Ads add a push, but they do not replace the basics. We explain the signals in our guide to how the Zomato ranking algorithm decides who gets seen.

  • Cuisine tags. Tag what customers actually search for. A biryani-led kitchen tagged only as "North Indian" can miss biryani searches.
  • Operating hours. Set hours you can keep. Going offline in peak slots costs orders you never see in your reports.
  • Delivery time. Long estimated times push customers to the next card. Prep time is the part you control.

Lever 2: Listing and photos

Zomato search is visual. Customers scroll a list of cards and tap the ones that look right. Your cover image, brand name, rating and visible offer decide whether they open your menu at all.

  • Use a clear, well-lit cover image of your hero dish, not the shopfront.
  • Add real photos to your best sellers first. Top-down shots on plain backgrounds work well on a phone screen.
  • Write short dish descriptions: portion, key ingredients, spice level. This cuts "not as expected" complaints later.
  • Keep the brand name simple and searchable.

Our Zomato listing optimization guide covers each field step by step.

Lever 3: Menu built for delivery

Most menus are built for dine-in. On a phone, a long menu slows the decision. A delivery menu should lead with best sellers, group items into a few clear categories and make a full meal one tap away.

  • Best sellers on top. Put the dishes that already convert where customers land.
  • Combos and meals. A main, side and drink bundle lifts order value without raising single-item prices.
  • Add-ons. Raita, extra gravy, desserts. Small, easy yes items.
  • Cut dead weight. Items that rarely sell add scroll time and prep complexity.

Menu conversion is measurable. At Balaji Dhaba in Hyderabad, Zomato menu-to-order conversion moved from 7.6% to 8.8% over the reported period, alongside visibility and ad work. The gap looks small, but it applies to every customer who opens the menu.

Lever 4: Pricing and offers

Price against similar restaurants in your delivery radius, not against your dine-in card. Your online price also has to cover commission, ad spend and discounts. Commission is not one fixed number. It depends on your contract, so check your own payout statement before setting prices.

Offers should do a job. Deep blanket discounts can buy volume but often wreck margin. Offers tied to a minimum order or a combo push customers to add more to the cart.

Lever 5: Zomato ads

Zomato's terms for its sponsored listing service define the fee as a cost per click, settled by deduction from your weekly payouts, and state that Zomato does not guarantee any minimum or maximum number of clicks. You pay for the click whether or not the customer orders. That is why ads on a weak listing burn money.

  • Fix availability, photos and menu before you scale spend.
  • Start small, read results weekly, and raise budgets only when returns hold.
  • Judge ads on cost per order and net sales, not impressions.
  • Expect early returns to settle as budgets grow. Olantic 8's Zomato ad return went from 9.3x on ₹398 of spend to 4.8x on ₹36.7K.

Across full PFC months in our 2026 growth benchmarks, platform-reported Zomato ad returns ranged from 1.7x to 9.3x. These are dashboard figures, not profit. To check your own numbers, divide ad spend by the orders it brought in, then compare that cost per order with what you keep per order after commission, using our commission calculator.

Lever 6: Ratings

Ratings affect both ranking and whether a customer taps your card. Customers give ratings, so no restaurant or consultant can set them directly. What you can do is remove the reasons behind bad reviews.

  • Cold or soggy food: better insulated, vented packaging.
  • Spills: sealed containers for gravies and liquids.
  • Wrong or missing items: a packing checklist at the pass.
  • Delays: realistic prep times and staffing for peak slots.

Reply to reviews politely and fix the issue raised. Never buy or fake reviews. It breaks platform rules and puts the listing at risk.

Lever 7: Operations and availability

This is the lever most owners skip, and often the biggest. A kitchen that goes offline, runs slow or rejects orders loses visibility and customers. Cancellations after you accept an order also cost you food, packaging and staff time, as Restaurant India explains in its June 2026 guide.

In our stronger client cases, outlets that scaled smoothly kept availability around 99%, cut prep time and pushed lost sales close to zero. At Olantic 8 in Gurugram, Zomato kitchen prep time fell from 26 to 11 minutes. At The Burrito Bros in Pune, it fell from 14 to 8 minutes.

How to increase Zomato orders: a weekly checklist

  1. Check impressions, menu opens and orders separately. Note which stage dropped.
  2. Review availability. Find the hours you went offline and why.
  3. Check average prep time and any rejected or cancelled orders.
  4. Confirm cuisine tags match what customers search for.
  5. Make sure every best seller has a clear, real photo and a short description.
  6. Compare your top five item prices with similar outlets in your radius.
  7. Run each live offer through a margin check and drop the ones that lose money.
  8. Read ad results on cost per order and net sales. Change budget only on returns.
  9. Read every low-rated review and log the cause: temperature, spill, wrong item or delay.
  10. Pick one fix for the coming week and give it an owner in the kitchen.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to increase Zomato orders?

It depends on where the leak is. Fixes to availability and prep time can show up within weeks, because they affect how often you are shown. Menu and photo changes need enough traffic to judge. Rating repair is slower, since it needs fresh reviews. In our client data, comparison windows run from one month to more than six. Results vary by outlet and are not guaranteed.

Do Zomato ads guarantee more orders?

No. Zomato's sponsored listing terms say it does not guarantee a minimum number of clicks, and you pay per click whether or not the customer orders. Ads work best on a listing that already converts well. Fix availability, photos and menu first, then scale spend only while cost per order and net sales hold up.

Should I give bigger discounts to get more Zomato orders?

Not by default. Deep discounts can lift volume but often cut margin to nothing, and customers start to expect them every time. Offers tied to a minimum order or a combo usually do more for order value. Check what you keep per order after commission, ads and discount before launching any offer, and review discount depth every month.

Why are my Zomato orders dropping when my rating is fine?

A good rating helps, but it is one signal among several. Check availability first. Going offline in peak hours, slow prep or rejected orders can reduce how often you are shown. Then check whether newer outlets nearby are pricing lower or running offers. Looking at impressions, menu opens and orders separately usually shows where the drop started.

Can a consultant change my Zomato rating or ranking directly?

No. Ratings come from customers and ranking is decided by Zomato. Anyone promising a fixed rating or a guaranteed top spot is best avoided. A good consultant works on the inputs: listing quality, menu, pricing, offers, ad efficiency and kitchen operations. Those improve the signals that the platform and customers respond to, without fake reviews or shortcuts.
