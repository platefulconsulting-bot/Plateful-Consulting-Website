A cloud kitchen is a commercial kitchen that cooks only for delivery. It has no dining area and no walk-in counter. Customers find it on apps like Swiggy and Zomato, or on its own website and WhatsApp. That is the simple cloud kitchen meaning: a food brand that lives online, served from a kitchen customers never see.

What is a cloud kitchen?

A cloud kitchen is a food business built around one channel: delivery. You rent a kitchen, get licences and list your brand on delivery apps. Orders come in on a tablet, the food is packed, and a delivery partner picks it up. Nobody sits down to eat.

You will hear several names for the same idea:

  • Dark kitchen or ghost kitchen: the kitchen itself, with no storefront.
  • Virtual brand: a menu and brand name that exists only on apps. One kitchen can run several virtual brands.
  • Delivery-only kitchen: the plain description.
  • Internet restaurant: the term Rebel Foods uses for its brands on its own website.

The meaning of cloud kitchen, then, is about the operating model, not the cuisine. A biryani brand, a pizza brand and a healthy-bowl brand can all be cloud kitchens. What they share is that the customer's whole experience is the app listing, the packaging and the food.

How is a cloud kitchen different from a restaurant, takeaway and home kitchen?

The biggest difference is where the money goes. A restaurant pays for a good location, seating, interiors and service staff. A cloud kitchen saves most of that, but it pays platform commission and spends on visibility inside the apps instead.

What are the main cloud kitchen models in India?

Most cloud kitchens in India fall into one of five models. They differ in who owns the kitchen, who owns the brand, and how many brands share one stove.

Independent single-brand. This is where most owners start. You control the menu and the brand. The risk is that one weak rating or one bad month hits your only income line.

Multi-brand from one kitchen. Rebel Foods is the best-known Indian example. It started as Faasos and now runs a large portfolio of delivery brands, including Behrouz Biryani, Oven Story Pizza, Sweet Truth and The Good Bowl. Inc42's December 2025 review of Rebel Foods reports 45+ brands, a presence in about 75 Indian cities, and a 2025 shift towards a hybrid model with physical outlets for some brands. The same report shows the company still made a net loss in FY25, which is a useful reminder that scale alone does not make a cloud kitchen profitable.

Kitchen-as-a-service. Here a provider builds kitchens and leases them out, often with shared utilities. It lowers your setup effort. In 2023 Swiggy sold its Access kitchen-infrastructure business to Kitchens@ in a share-swap deal, so this space is now run by independent operators rather than the apps.

Franchise brands. You pay for a known name, recipes and supply chain. Whether that is worth it depends on the terms. Our breakdown of cloud kitchen franchises in India covers what to check.

Restaurant-run delivery-only brands. This is often the lowest-risk model. The kitchen, staff and licences already exist. You add a second or third brand on the apps to use idle capacity in the afternoon or late night.

Is there a Zomato cloud kitchen or Swiggy cloud kitchen?

People often search for "Zomato cloud kitchen" expecting Zomato to give them a kitchen. Today, neither app runs kitchens for restaurant partners. Zomato wound down its own pilot cloud kitchen in 2018, and Swiggy sold Access Kitchens in 2023. Both now list cloud kitchens as restaurant partners, just like any dine-in outlet. You register, sign the partner contract and appear on the app under your brand name.

How does a cloud kitchen get orders?

A cloud kitchen has no footfall, so every order has to be earned on a screen. These are the main sources.

  • Swiggy and Zomato. For most kitchens these bring the bulk of orders. You pay a commission on each order, plus optional spends on ads and discounts. Commission is a percentage that depends on your contract, city and services you take, so it is best quoted as a range. You can model your own numbers with our commission calculator.
  • ONDC. The Open Network for Digital Commerce is a government-backed open network. A restaurant listed on it can be found through several different buyer apps instead of one platform. For most kitchens it brings far fewer orders than Swiggy and Zomato today, so treat it as a supplement.
  • Newer delivery apps. Some new players run their own apps outside ONDC. Rapido's Ownly is one example. Outlook Business reported in July 2026 that Ownly charges restaurants no commission and uses a subscription model instead, and that it has moved into the main Rapido app. Check the current terms and the order volume in your area before you count on it.
  • Own channels. A WhatsApp number, Instagram page, simple website or office tie-ups. Margins are better because there is no commission, but you handle delivery and marketing yourself.

The apps decide who gets seen using ratings, delivery times, photos, prices and conversion. Ignore these and even good food will struggle.

What are the pros and cons of a cloud kitchen?

Pros

  • Lower setup cost than a dine-in restaurant, since you skip interiors and seating.
  • Location can be a back lane or upper floor, as long as riders can reach it fast.
  • You can test a menu or a new brand quickly and shut it down cheaply if it fails.
  • One kitchen can serve several brands and cuisines.

Cons

  • Heavy dependence on two platforms and their rules.
  • Commission, ads and discounts eat into every order.
  • No walk-in customers to fall back on when app orders dip.
  • Your brand is only as strong as your rating and packaging.
  • Competition is intense, because the barrier to entry is low.

Who does a cloud kitchen suit?

A cloud kitchen suits people who enjoy running operations and reading numbers more than hosting guests. It works well for:

  • Chefs or home cooks with a focused menu that travels well.
  • Existing restaurants with spare kitchen time.
  • Operators who want to test a cuisine before investing in a dine-in outlet.
  • Founders with a limited budget who can still afford a few months of runway.

It suits people less if they expect orders to arrive just because they are listed, or if their food goes soggy or cold within 30 minutes.

Cloud kitchen economics in plain terms

A cloud kitchen's cost structure has three layers. First, setup: rent deposit, equipment, exhaust, gas line, licences and initial stock. Second, fixed monthly costs: rent, salaries, utilities and software. Third, per-order costs: ingredients, packaging, platform commission, discounts and ads.

Many new owners plan the first two layers carefully and underestimate the third. In our experience, per-order costs decide whether a kitchen survives. Rent and equipment costs also vary a lot by city and area. For indicative ranges and how to plan them, see our guide to cloud kitchen setup cost in India.

PFC's founder, Deepak Desh Bandhu, ran a bootstrapped cloud kitchen from 2019 to 2021, through the COVID period. The lesson that stayed with him is simple: the kitchen that tracks its per-order margin every week is the one that lasts.

How to start a cloud kitchen: a quick checklist

  1. Pick one cuisine and a short menu that holds up after 30 minutes in a box.
  2. Check demand in your delivery radius by studying competing brands on both apps.
  3. Choose a location for rider access and delivery reach, not for street visibility.
  4. Get your FSSAI registration or licence, GST if applicable, and local trade licence. Our licence and registration checklist lists them.
  5. Set up the kitchen with only the equipment your menu actually needs.
  6. Price each dish after counting commission, packaging and discounts, not just food cost.
  7. Register on Swiggy and Zomato with good photos and clear dish descriptions.
  8. Watch ratings, cancellations and per-order margin weekly for the first three months.

Frequently asked questions

What is the meaning of cloud kitchen in simple words?

A cloud kitchen is a restaurant without a dining room. It prepares food only for delivery and gets orders through apps like Swiggy and Zomato or its own channels. Customers never visit the kitchen. They judge the brand only by its online listing, ratings, packaging and the food that arrives. Other names include dark kitchen, ghost kitchen and delivery-only kitchen.

Does Zomato or Swiggy run cloud kitchens for restaurant owners?

Not today. Zomato wound down its own pilot cloud kitchen back in 2018, and Swiggy sold its Access Kitchens business to Kitchens@ in 2023. Both platforms now treat cloud kitchens as regular restaurant partners. You need your own kitchen, or a unit rented from a shared-kitchen provider, and then you register on each app separately under your brand name.

Can one cloud kitchen run more than one brand?

Yes. Many cloud kitchens run two or more virtual brands from the same space, each with its own menu and listing. Rebel Foods is the best-known Indian example of this approach. It works when the brands share ingredients and equipment, and when your team can handle the extra menu without slowing preparation or hurting ratings on any single brand.

Is a cloud kitchen cheaper than a restaurant?

Usually the setup is cheaper, because you skip seating, interiors and a high-street location. The running costs work differently, not always lower. A cloud kitchen pays commission on every app order and often spends on ads and discounts to get seen. Whether it is more profitable depends on your menu pricing, order volume and how well you control per-order costs.

Do I need a separate licence for a cloud kitchen?

A cloud kitchen needs the same core approvals as any food business: FSSAI registration or licence based on turnover, GST registration where it applies, and local permissions such as a trade licence and fire clearance depending on your city. There is no special "cloud kitchen licence". Requirements vary by state and municipality, so check with your local authority before you sign a rent agreement.
