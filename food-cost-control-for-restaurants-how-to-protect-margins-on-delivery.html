Restaurants protect delivery margins on Swiggy and Zomato by keeping food cost at roughly 28–35% of selling price and controlling the four things that push it up: dish-level costing, portion size, wastage and supplier pricing. Commission, packaging and discounts already eat into every delivery order, so tight food cost is what leaves room for profit.

Key methods include:

  • calculating dish-level food cost regularly
  • standardising portion sizes
  • optimising vendor pricing
  • tracking wastage patterns
  • designing menu items with strong contribution margin

Restaurants that actively manage food cost can improve profit margin by around 5–15% per order, even without raising prices (illustrative; results vary and are not guaranteed).

Delivery platforms amplify margin pressure because of commission costs, which makes food cost control essential for profitability.

Why Food Cost Control Matters More on Delivery Platforms

Delivery orders carry additional cost layers compared to dine-in operations.

Key cost components include:

  • platform commission: Swiggy typically 18–27% and Zomato 18–28% base commission in contracts PFC audits; press reports put the range at roughly 16–30% (Angel One news report)
  • packaging cost
  • discount contribution
  • ad spend allocation

Without food cost control, restaurants may generate high revenue but low profit.

Example: a ₹400 order with 35% food cost means ₹140 goes on ingredients. After commission and packaging, the margin left may be minimal.

Food cost discipline keeps profitability sustainable.

Calculating Food Cost Per Dish

Dish-level costing shows which menu items actually make money.

Food Cost Formula

Food cost % = (total ingredient cost ÷ selling price) × 100

  • low-margin items

  • menu optimisation needs

Portion Control for Consistency

Portion variation is one of the most common causes of food cost fluctuation. Even small inconsistencies affect margins.

Example

If a dish's standard portion cost is ₹120 but the portion actually served costs ₹140, profit margin falls significantly across multiple orders.

Portion Control Best Practices

  • standardise recipe quantities
  • use measuring tools
  • train kitchen staff
  • define portion guidelines

Consistency improves both cost control and customer experience.

Wastage Tracking & Inventory Discipline

Ingredient wastage directly pushes up food cost percentage.

Common causes include:

  • over-ordering inventory
  • improper storage
  • poor demand forecasting
  • expired ingredients

Vendor Pricing Strategy

Supplier pricing shapes your long-term cost structure. Even small price differences add up across a month of orders.

Vendor Optimisation Approach

  • compare supplier pricing regularly
  • negotiate bulk purchase rates
  • maintain multiple vendor options
  • check ingredient quality consistency

A balanced vendor strategy improves cost efficiency without hurting quality.

Menu Engineering & Food Cost Relationship

Food cost analysis helps you decide what belongs on the menu.

High-performing menus typically include:

  • high-margin items
  • ingredient overlap across dishes
  • scalable recipes

Ingredient overlap reduces inventory complexity. For example, using a similar base gravy across multiple dishes improves efficiency.

A balanced cost structure keeps pricing stable.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good food cost percentage for restaurants in India?

Most restaurants aim for 28–35% food cost depending on cuisine and pricing strategy. Cloud kitchens usually target 28–32% and QSRs 25–30%.

How does delivery affect food cost?

Delivery adds packaging and commission costs, so food cost needs tighter control. Swiggy typically charges 18–27% and Zomato 18–28% base commission in contracts PFC audits; press reports put the range at roughly 16–30% (Angel One news report).

How often should I update food cost sheets?

Monthly menu costing updates are recommended, with weekly inventory checks and a quarterly vendor price review.

Should all dishes have the same food cost percentage?

No. Some dishes may carry a higher cost but still improve overall menu balance.

Does menu size affect food cost?

Yes. Larger menus often increase wastage and inventory complexity.
