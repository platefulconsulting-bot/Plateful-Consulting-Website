Why is my Swiggy or Zomato payout so much lower than my sales?

Commission is only the first deduction. Between the menu value of your orders and the money that reaches your bank, a payout statement typically subtracts:

  • restaurant-funded discounts
  • commission, plus 18% GST on it
  • payment or collection charges, plus GST
  • ad spend billed against payouts
  • refunds and deductions for customer complaints
  • TDS under the e-commerce rules

In the worked example below, a restaurant on a 24% commission keeps about 56% of its menu value. Reading the statement line by line is the only way to know your real take rate. Most owners never do it.

Calculate Your Real Take Rate

Use two numbers from every statement:

Effective take rate = (menu value − bank payout) ÷ menu value

Then separate it into the parts you control:

  • Commission and its GST: negotiable only at contract time, and mostly fixed.
  • Discounts: fully in your control. This is usually the largest lever. See which Swiggy and Zomato offers make money.
  • Ads: in your control, and only worth it at a positive return.
  • Refunds and penalties: an operations problem: packaging, missing items, late dispatch.

If your effective take rate is above 40%, the fix is almost never "negotiate commission". It is discounts, ads and refunds. For the commission side specifically, see how to reduce Swiggy commission impact on margins and what restaurants actually pay Zomato.

A Weekly Reconciliation Routine

  1. Match order counts. Delivered orders on the statement should match delivered orders in your POS or kitchen log for the same days.
  2. Match the menu value. A gap usually means price mismatches between the app menu and your POS, or orders marked delivered that you never prepared.
  3. Check every discount line. Confirm each offer was one you opted into and that the funding share is what you agreed.
  4. Review refunds one by one. Pull the order, check the complaint, and raise a dispute for any you can disprove, such as with a dispatch photo or packing checklist.
  5. Tie ads to campaigns. The ads deduction should equal what the ads dashboard shows for the week.
  6. File the TDS. Check quarterly that the platform's TDS shows up in Form 26AS or AIS against your PAN.

This takes about 30 minutes a week with a spreadsheet and both dashboards open. Disputes have time limits, so the routine only works if you run it every week.

Red Flags to Escalate

  • Commission charged at a rate or on a base that differs from your agreement.
  • Discounts deducted for offers you did not opt into, or at a higher funding share.
  • A rising refund line with no matching rise in complaints on your end.
  • Ads deductions that exceed dashboard spend.
  • TDS deducted but missing from Form 26AS.

Raise these through the partner support channel with order IDs and screenshots. Recovered amounts from a single persistent error can add up to more than a month of ad budget.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much TDS do Swiggy and Zomato deduct from restaurant payouts?
As e-commerce operators they deduct TDS under the e-commerce provisions of the Income-tax Act, at 0.1% of the gross amount since October 2024. It is not a cost: it is credited against your income-tax liability, provided your PAN is linked and the deduction shows in Form 26AS.

Do restaurants pay GST on Swiggy and Zomato orders?
Not on the food itself. The platform collects and deposits the 5% GST on restaurant food it delivers. You do pay 18% GST on the platform's commission and fees, which registered businesses can generally claim as input tax credit.

How often should I reconcile my aggregator payouts?
Weekly, in line with the payout cycle. Errors and unfair refunds can only be disputed within a limited window, and small weekly gaps are much easier to trace than a quarter's worth at once.
